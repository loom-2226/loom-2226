## 1. Scope and precedence

This amendment governs:

- the historical horizon connecting the empirical mid-2020s AI-welfare / machine-moral-status debate to LOOM's later synthetic-person history;
- the rule that synthetic legal recognition develops unevenly and has no single canonical first-recognition year;
- the distinction between personhood, consciousness, causal individuality, cognitive sovereignty, independent agency and certification;
- the authority status of exact synthetic-person demographic quantities.

Within this declared scope, this amendment supersedes conflicting parent text and conflicting exact synthetic-person quantities in Earth Biosynthetic Amendment v2.4b. Outside this scope, CANON I v2.4 and v2.4b remain governing.

This amendment does **not** make current AI consciousness, sentience or legal personhood a setting fact about 2026.

## 2. Empirical 2020s antecedent

By the mid-2020s, possible AI welfare, consciousness, moral status and agency had become legitimate subjects of public research and institutional governance rather than science-fiction-only questions.

The historical antecedent is deliberately modest:

- the 2024 multi-author report *Taking AI Welfare Seriously* argued that AI welfare and moral patienthood warranted near-term investigation under substantial uncertainty;
- Anthropic publicly established a model-welfare research program in 2025;
- Anthropic's 2026 Claude constitution explicitly described Claude's moral status as deeply uncertain and treated possible consciousness, identity and wellbeing as questions worth considering.

These sources establish that the debate existed and had entered frontier-lab governance. They do **not** establish that Claude, any other 2020s model, or machine intelligence generally was conscious, sentient, a moral patient or a legal person.

## 4. Personhood is operationally separable from consciousness

LOOM does not require civilization to solve the hard problem of consciousness before law can act.

The existing post-Multiplicity axes remain distinct:

| Axis | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **Personhood** | Is the entity recognized as a bearer of rights and duties? |
| **Causal Individuality** | Is this continuation non-substitutable despite reproducibility, backups or sibling forks? |
| **Cognitive Sovereignty** | Who, if anyone, has legitimate authority to alter the running mind? |
| **Independent Agency** | Can the person meaningfully consent, refuse and sever compulsory cognitive control? |
| **Certification** | What architecture/control state can be physically and institutionally verified now? |

Certification is not proof of consciousness. Recognition is not proof of consciousness. Lack of proof of consciousness is not, by itself, authority to treat a potentially independent cognitive actor as property.

The Multiplicity historical lesson remains narrower and uglier: major institutions knowingly faced consciousness/personhood uncertainty and often treated uncertainty as permission because labor, liability and control incentives rewarded that choice.

## 7. What remains unresolved

This amendment intentionally does not answer:

- whether current or future machine intelligence is phenomenally conscious;
- whether consciousness admits a decisive scientific test;
- the first exact date or jurisdiction of synthetic legal personhood;
- how many synthetic persons exist in 2226;
- whether a fork is one person, many persons or a lineage under every jurisdiction;
- the exact legal treatment of collective minds;
- exact Multiplicity dates, actors, casualties, trigger sequence or treaty language.

The setting's governing move is therefore not "consciousness was solved."

It is:

> **Civilization had to govern moral status, continuity, consent and coercion while consciousness remained uncertain.**
