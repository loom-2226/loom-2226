# Earth 2226 reference baseline — frozen 23 September 2026

**Decision B: retain the existing trajectory.** This is a reproducible **exploratory reference scenario** for 80 modeled economies, not an empirically validated forecast or an exhaustive world total. The reference is the already qualified `v0.6.1-d1-c1-h1-r1` run with policy `UNTREATED_FROZEN_2060_EXPLICIT_v1`; this finalization adds no model code, coefficient, simulation, duplicate endpoint or checkpoint. `BASELINE_MANIFEST.json` pins the inputs, executable files, all previously recorded run products and their SHA-256 hashes in place.

The starting state is the previously software-qualified, frozen **2026** country/sector/asset seed, linked through the repaired 2026–2031 bridge and 2031–2060 stage to the successor's frozen **2060** boundary. The long-run runner then records 2061–2226 annual transitions. The seed combines observed or modelled historical sources with WPP 2024's **2026 projection**, PWT 11.0 factor shares through 2023, reconstructed capital, and **projected 2024** OECD sector/trade input. Its arithmetic qualification does not make all its components contemporaneous observations. The prior successor preserved replacement-first investment accounting: underfunded replacement produces exactly zero expansion. The rejected D1 dynamic-investment candidate was never integrated; investment/value-added ratios remain country-specific constants.

## Capital-exponent decision

The recovered 2026 bridge selects the latest valid PWT `labsh` through 2023 and computes `alpha = 1 − labsh`; 72 economies have direct workbook values and eight use a declared target-country median fallback. The audited 80-country vector matches the active frozen 2060 vector, and the runner uses it in `Y_s = T A_s K_s^alpha L_s^(1−alpha)`. Nigeria has `alpha = 0.68844273686409` and fixed investment/VA `0.30704124547710465`; Angola has `alpha = 0.776764899492264` and investment/VA `0.10384533241310046`. Source mapping and active use are verified. The original PWT program labels `labsh` as labor compensation's share of GDP **at current national prices**, not as a measured 200-year production elasticity. Under restrictive competitive, constant-returns and complete-factor assumptions, an income share can proxy an elasticity; those assumptions are not established here. Resource rents and mixed income are especially relevant: PWT's methodological note explicitly discusses them for Saudi Arabia ([PWT note](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/docs/what_is_new_in_pwt90.pdf)). Holding the 2023-derived exponent fixed through 2226 is a consequential scenario assumption, **not** an independently qualified economic law.

The same source test was applied to all 80. Among 72 direct PWT cases, 18 have a latest `labsh` value identical for at least five consecutive years and 10 for at least ten; the workbook alone does not show whether each repetition is observation or imputation. Nigeria's 0.31155726313591 and Saudi Arabia's 0.279617875814438 are identical for **2009–2023**; Côte d’Ivoire's is identical for **2013–2023**. Angola's latest run is 2021–2023, Ireland's only 2023, and China's 2021–2023. These dates qualify the source evidence, but do not demonstrate that the value was parsed incorrectly or identify a replacement elasticity. A high alpha alone does not imply explosive output: Saudi Arabia's 2060–2226 value added falls while Nigeria and Angola compound strongly. The 80-economy audit found no failed production, capital or aggregation identity caused by alpha; high long-run sensitivity is not a numerical instability or implementation defect.

The original **September 2** alpha-only sensitivity is recovered in `LOOM_SOLAR_CIVPROP_2060_2190_IQR_WINSORIZED_ALPHA_Sensitivity_v0.1.py` and its saved 2190 report/outputs. It freezes the **2060** state, replaces alpha only from **2061** with two-sided cross-country IQR bounds **0.416310876607895–0.5106692910194399**, and adjusts sector `A` by `(K_2060/L_2060)^(alpha_old−alpha_new)` to preserve boundary output. Forty of 80 exponents change. Its corresponding original and alpha-only saved 2190 runs have exactly equal population for all 80 and TFP equal to within `1e−12`; they show strong **within-archive** sensitivity. The alpha-only 2190 result changes Nigeria's VA from **287.979 to 66.675 trillion** proxy units and Angola's from **28.478 to 5.261 trillion**, but raises Saudi Arabia's from **0.731 to 0.957 trillion**; China's alpha is unchanged and its VA changes through cross-country interactions. These effects do not prove which exponent is economically correct. The bounds are distributional quartiles, not observed elasticities; the archived governance says `deployment_performed: false` and `2226_endpoint_used: false`. Its old runner also predates the successor's replacement-shortfall fix and ends in **2190**. No archived 2226 sensitivity is a controlled comparison against this qualified successor's full demographic and code lineage. We therefore neither adopt the IQR bounds nor substitute a common median.

This is a bounded decision, not a declaration that fixed alpha is empirically sound. No specific wrong source value, code path, unit conversion, failed invariant or independently estimated replacement exponent was demonstrated. Altering alpha now would introduce an unqualified assumption and require renormalizing `A` to avoid a level shock. The existing trajectory is retained with explicit sensitivity and long-run validity limits.

## Frozen result and qualification

At **2226**, the 80 modeled economies contain **6,572,523,389.641058 persons**, produce **950,281,790,828,375.9** annual value-added proxy units, hold **5,825,194,995,478,961** capital-stock proxy units and invest **263,918,322,985,392.94** proxy units annually. Nigeria produces **441.824 trillion** annual VA, **46.494%** of the model total; Angola produces **54.421 trillion**. These monetary numbers inherit a constant-2015-USD **scale**, not validated 2226 dollar purchasing power. Sector gross output includes intermediate turnover and is not added to value added.

Previously recorded qualification: the 2026–2031 bridge passed; 2060–2226 annual hard gates passed with no failures; 80 countries, 800 sectors and 3,200 assets reconcile at the endpoint; four focused replacement/provenance regression tests passed; **zero** expansion allocations occurred in 2,626 replacement-shortfall country-years. A fresh process resumed the complete 2225 checkpoint and produced byte-identical 2226 country/sector/asset endpoint files and the same decoded state fingerprint. The historical v0.5 all-years 2031→2032 investment-share gate remains **REVIEW** (4.009355 percentage-point move versus 3); the separately specified v0.2 2034–2060 boundary gate remains **PASS**. This finalization reverified preserved file hashes, the 80-country parameter vector and output counts; it did **not** rerun trajectories or promote REVIEW to PASS.

Material limitations remain: fixed alpha and investment/VA through two centuries; projected OECD 2024 input and mixed-year 2026 seed; reconstructed capital and sector-asset allocation; authored post-2100 demographic tail; calibrated TFP convergence; no migration market or physical resource/energy closure. D1's withheld historical investment test failed, so there is no validated dynamic replacement. The scenario is numerically qualified under its inherited gates, while its distant economic distribution—including Nigeria's concentration—remains uncertain.
