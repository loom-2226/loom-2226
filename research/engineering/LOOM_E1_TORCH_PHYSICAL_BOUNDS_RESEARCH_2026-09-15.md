## External anchors

### PFRC / Direct Fusion Drive scale

Princeton/PPPL Direct Fusion Drive literature describes a D-3He high-beta field-reversed-configuration concept with a linear magnetic geometry, thrust augmentation by adding propellant/remass to the scrape-off-layer flow, and a magnetic nozzle.

The 2018 NIAC/PPPL Pluto study describes a scaled PFRC reactor as roughly 4–8 m long and 1.5 m diameter, producing 1–10 MW, and reports mission feasibility contingent on engine specific power of roughly 750–1250 W/kg.

The 2019 PPPL/JBIS interstellar study states then-current estimated DFD specific power of roughly 0.3–1.5 kW/kg. It treats much higher values as requiring revolutionary improvements; examples include 25 kW/kg for a 500-year Alpha Centauri rendezvous case and 100 kW/kg-class illustrative interstellar cases.

A PPPL teaching presentation gives a Pluto reference case of 2 MW fusion power, 0.7 kW/kg specific power, thrust efficiency 0.5, and engine mass about 2857 kg.

Princeton Satellite Systems' 2020 Mars mission example uses 40 MW and 1.2 kW/kg specific power with about 110 km/s exhaust velocity. Their later deliberately extreme 1-g Mars thought experiment finds that multi-terawatt propulsion would require order 1e8 W/kg engine specific power for a useful payload fraction; PSS explicitly calls such numbers not remotely plausible with known technology and contrasts them with their DFD estimate around 1 MW/tonne (1e3 W/kg).

The 2022 Titan PFRC/DFD paper states a practical lower exhaust-velocity limit around 70 km/s for its modeled assumptions and explicitly uses a 0.4 power-to-thrust conversion efficiency in that context. This is not a Wayfarer efficiency.

### Wayfarer comparison

The qualified E1 performance envelope requires direct kinetic jet power from about 5.112 TW (ECON) to 12.781 TW (HARD/LIMIT). This is roughly six orders of magnitude above the 1–10 MW class of the published DFD research analog.

Using specific power only as a scaling diagnostic, and unrealistically giving the source credit for exactly the jet power with no additional losses:

- at 1.5 kW/kg, 12.781 TW corresponds to about 8.52 million tonnes of source system;
- at 25 kW/kg, about 511 thousand tonnes;
- at 100 kW/kg, about 128 thousand tonnes;
- at 1 MW/kg, about 12,781 tonnes;
- at 10 MW/kg, about 1,278 tonnes;
- at 100 MW/kg, about 128 tonnes.

The last value is the order of magnitude needed merely to place a LIMIT-mode ~12.8 TW source inside a ~100-tonne-class propulsion allocation, before accounting for directed-energy fraction below unity, shielding, remass handling, nozzle/magnets, thermal systems, structure, driver/recirculating power, lifetime margins, or auxiliaries.

Therefore present DFD/PFRC specific-power estimates cannot be scaled directly into Wayfarer. Wayfarer requires a qualitatively enormous specific-power advance or a different source/propulsion architecture.

## What the literature does support

It supports retaining a D-He3 or related low-neutron high-beta FRC/direct-fusion-drive family as a research direction because the topology is relevant: linear reactor, charged fusion products, remass augmentation, variable thrust/specific impulse, and magnetic nozzle.

It does not support assigning Wayfarer a numerical directed-energy fraction, fusion Q, reactor specific power, nozzle efficiency, deposition ppm, or physical plume.

## Engineering consequences

1. Source-system specific power is a first-order kill criterion, not a secondary optimization.
2. Physical energy partition must be separated into directed exhaust, neutral radiation, vehicle deposition, recirculating/driver requirements, and other losses.
3. The 160 t candidate propulsion allocation cannot be treated as closed until source, shield, magnets/nozzle, feed, structure and thermal mass are reconciled.
4. A present-day DFD-like architecture survives only as a topology/research analog unless a governed 2226 technology-development path earns orders-of-magnitude improvements.
5. The engineering model should expose the improvement factor relative to multiple external anchors rather than select one optimistic future value.
