## Executive finding

The pressure sphere and hot mechanisms are plausible technology *families*; no available evidence qualifies the combined Venus surface–orbit round trip. The first-order unknowns are **ascent energy/water, thrust power source, exhaust speed at useful thrust, and thermal protection during surface residence**. The proposed 760 kN water thruster at 9 km/s has **3.42 GW ideal kinetic jet power**, so **3.4 GW electrical input cannot supply it at any efficiency below unity**. Historical output 7.7 GW is a different, lost model and cannot resolve this. A second problem is the **unknown v1 total propulsion-water capacity**. The **23.1 t capacity is a historical LOST-sizer output**, not a v1 input, so a v1 rocket-equation delta-v cannot be calculated yet. Venus low circular-orbit speed is ~7.3 km/s, not an ascent budget: trajectory, aerodynamic lift, gravity/drag losses and separately defined engine modes must be solved. The power check is **DERIVED CONDITIONAL** from unqualified v1 values, not rejection of the mission objective.

## Q3: current evidence and 2226 opportunity ranges

### (a) Venus hardware: Venera, VeGa, LLISSE, HOTTech, SiC/GaN, hot actuators and seals

**ONLINE / high:** Venus near-surface **~89–94 bar, 735–737 K**, dense ~65 kg/m³ CO2. Soviet Venera 13 transmitted **127 min**, Venera 14 **57 min**; VeGa 1/2 balloons returned ~**46.5 h** at cloud altitude while surface landers survived **~56–57 min**. NASA Glenn SiC circuits survived **521 h** in simulated Venus gas and later **60 days** in simulated conditions; this is electronics, **not a crewed lander**. HOTTech targets **500°C for ≥60 days**, and Honeybee tested extreme-temperature gearing at **486°C**; seals, bearings, bulkhead feedthroughs, wheels and repeated-duty qualification remain integration problems. GaN transistor work reports **11 days** in 460°C/94 bar Venus-like gas; SiC and GaN are not whole-spacecraft proof.

**ONLINE range:** historical lander service **~0.95–2.12 h**; tested hot circuit endurance **~521–1,440 h** in differing conditions; hot environment **~89–94 bar / 735–737 K**. **PROJECTION / medium-low:** hot-side sensors/actuators can move out of chilled cabin, but no numeric mass saving until inventories are partitioned. **v1 verdict:** *supports* distributed hot controls; *leaves open* 18 h cold-water stay, seals, cycle life, crew-egress and return. Sources: [NASA Venus facts](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html), [Venus mission compilation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6839549/), [NASA SiC](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/science-enabling-technology/technology-highlights/integrated-circuits-to-enable-exploration-of-the-harshest-environments-in-solar-system/), [HOTTech](https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/pesto/hottech/), [gearhead](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/8984), [GaN experiment](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0186976).

### (b) External-pressure crew sphere: titanium, ceramic, glass

**ONLINE / high:** Alvin's certified human submersible has a titanium **2.1 m inside-diameter sphere**, **7.4 cm wall**, **4.8 m³ personnel volume**, operated to **6,500 m** depth; entire Alvin mass **~20.4 t** must **not** be assigned to the sphere. Deep-ocean pressure hull safety investigations underscore buckling/imperfections, repeated external-pressure loads, windows and interfaces; neither glass nor ceramic is established as a reusable, penetrated, crew-rated shuttle sphere.

**PROJECTION / low screening only:** For **2.8 m OD Ti** and **~9 MPa external differential**, shell **~0.03–0.07 m** and corresponding ideal geometric Ti shell **~3.2–7.6 t** using 4,430 kg/m³. This is *not* a pressure/buckling-rated thickness or mass (no welds, openings, load paths, safety factor, heat-creep or hot fatigue). **OPEN:** cabin mass (the lost sizer's value), rover/cabin mating structure, hot pressure cycling, pressure seals and window scheme. **v1 verdict:** *supports* sphere topology, *leaves open* mass and 4-person crew practicality. Sources: [WHOI Alvin specifications](https://ndsf.whoi.edu/alvin/specifications/), [WHOI FAQ](https://www.whoi.edu/what-we-do/explore/underwater-vehicles/hov-alvin/faqs/), [TSB pressure-hull investigation](https://tsb-bst.gc.ca/eng/medias-media/fiches-facts/m23a0169/m23a0169-20260617-03.html). Screening equations: shell mass ~4πr²tρ; external-pressure buckling **cannot** be certified by this relation.

### (c) Insulation in ~9 MPa supercritical CO2 at 735 K

**ONLINE / high:** NASA ALIVE/COMPASS insulated the **interior of the pressure vessel with aerogel** and modeled ~**735 K outside, 300 K inside**, plus heat leakage through supports, wiring and windows. Vacuum multilayer insulation is **not** a directly transferrable exterior solution in dense supercritical gas: gas conduction and ingress defeat vacuum assumptions unless a sealed evacuated interspace is actually supplied.

**ASSUMPTION / low sensitivity only:** internal aerogel/porous insulation *effective* conductivity **0.02–0.10 W/(m·K)** and thickness **0.05–0.15 m** (not a Venus-tested material curve); **2.8 m OD** sphere area ~**24.6 m²** implies ideal conduction ~**1.4–20.9 kW** across 435 K depending on joint choices, **before** penetrations, atmosphere convection, heat stores, and inside equipment. **v1 verdict:** *leaves open* thermal kit mass and 18 h/3–6 h claims; conservative thermal leakage is potentially many kW, not automatically a tiny ice-pack load. Source: [NASA ALIVE COMPASS Final Report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160011272). Numerical sensitivity is explicitly a model input, **not** attributed to NASA.

### (d) Active refrigeration on Venus: Stirling

**ONLINE / medium-high:** ALIVE studied duplex Stirling with **~300 W cooling** while consuming **~1,500 W electrical for the cooler**, using a high-temperature heat engine/burner and hot-side rejection. The study used **~35% of Carnot** cooler performance as an assumption; not a demonstrated 2026 crew module. At 300 K cold / 735 K hot, ideal Carnot refrigerator COP **0.69**. 

**PROJECTION / low-to-medium screening:** real COP **~0.07–0.24** for **10–35% of Carnot** assumed, equivalent to **~4.1–14.5 kW electrical input per kW lifted**, plus rejected heat of **~5.1–15.5 kW per kW lifted** at hot end. Do not count Stirling as heat elimination. **v1 verdict:** *challenges* cold-water-only endurance unless the measured leakage, hot-side heat rejection, and stored-energy/water integration close. **OPEN:** cold-water heat capacity and temperature window, cooling duty, refrigeration mass, and true 18 h/3–6 h thermal endurance. Source: [NASA ALIVE](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160011272). This source is a 2012 concept, not flight-qualified hardware.

### (e) Direct Fusion Drive/FRC and remass augmentation

**ONLINE / medium:** Princeton/PPPL Direct Fusion Drive studies describe charged-product energy plus injected propellant, magnetic exhaust, and modeled **~2.5–5 N per MW**, **~10,000 s specific impulse (~98 km/s)** for MW-class systems. Research already in repository cites **~70–110 km/s** example exhaust velocities and very low specific power compared to a compact shuttle multi-GW/TW source. They establish a **physical architectural analogue**, not a scalable 2226 engine.

**PROJECTION / very low, explicit scenarios only:** augmented shuttle torch low-mode **10–25 km/s**, high-mode **25–100 km/s** are **unearned sensitivity intervals**, not ONLINE results. At fixed thrust, power grows with ve. **OPEN:** all six lost inputs relevant here (TORCH150 meaning, ve, thrust, torch length, propulsion/structure mass), reactor source power, directed-fusion partition, plume, coil/shield mass, ability to run a mode in Venus air. **v1 verdict:** *compatible as a hypothesis* with ~14.5/40 km/s, but **does not support either numeric mode**. Sources: [NASA DFD tech project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/93994), [E1 physical-bounds review](LOOM_E1_TORCH_PHYSICAL_BOUNDS_RESEARCH_2026-09-15.md).

### (f) GW-class water thermal/arcjet/MPD thrusters

**ONLINE / high on scale distinction:** laboratory MPD examples exist around **100 kW**, with a 2025 superconducting AF-MPD study reporting **up to ~71% efficiency** at a tested operating point. NASA water/CO2-oriented rotating-magnetic-field thruster work notes research-prototype efficiency **~1%** in one family. None proves an **atmosphere-operating GW water engine**, a reusable nozzle array or dry mass.

**DERIVED CONDITIONAL:** ideal direct kinetic power Pjet = F·ve/2; mass flow = F/ve. At v1 **760 kN** and **9 km/s**: **3.42 GW ideal jet power** and **84.4 kg/s water**. At a **0.30–0.70** total electrical-to-jet efficiency **ASSUMPTION**: **~4.89–11.4 GW input** (not 3.4); the rejected/uncoupled power partition, hardware mass and cooling are **OPEN**. If higher mode ve **10–25 km/s** at the same 760 kN thrust, ideal jet power becomes **3.8–9.5 GW** and water flow **76–30.4 kg/s**. Nominal **1.6 t spare water** equals just **~19 s** at the 9 km/s/760 kN full-flow point; it must not be treated as generous ascent margin. **v1 verdict:** *contradicts* 3.4 GW **as input** at 760 kN/9 km/s; *leaves open* actual high-power system mass and near-surface thrust. Sources: [2025 AF-MPD](https://doi.org/10.1088/1674-1056/ad9910), [NASA rotating-magnetic-field study](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/118333), [1989 NASA AF-MPD](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19890018330).

### (g) Biconic aerodynamic lift and rolling takeoff: Venus / Titan

**ONLINE / high environment:** Venus ~**65 kg/m³** near surface, **~8.87 m/s²** gravity; Titan ~**4× Earth's sea-level atmospheric density** with **~1/7 Earth gravity**. Those facts make lift attractive but do not certify biconic ascent.

**ASSUMPTION / low screening:** Total v1 wet mass is **OPEN** because the original water capacity is lost; its 35.4 t dry mass is not a liftoff mass. If rolling liftoff occurs at **25–40 m/s** in **65 kg/m³** air, and effective CL **0.5–1.5** (explicitly *assumed*), dynamic-pressure lift requires **~0.11–0.87 m² effective lifting area per tonne of actual liftoff wet mass**. A stubby biconic body may need deployable surfaces; no qualified wing CL/CD, high-temperature tire, runway, pavement, surface-slope, aerodynamic steering, lateral loads, gas ingestion, or plume-safe roll path exists. The 760 kN thrust provides **~2.42× Venus weight at an unrealistically dry 35.4 t** and a strictly lower ratio when loaded; true thrust margin is **OPEN**. Titan rolling is likely mechanically easier in lift terms but requires 94 K materials/traction and different heat kit. **v1 verdict:** *supports investigating* rolling/belly-lift; *leaves open* attainable ascent-water reduction and recovery at an arbitrary landing site. Sources: [NASA Venus facts](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html), [NASA Dragonfly](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/why-is-nasa-sending-dragonfly-to-titan-here-are-five-reasons/), [2026 Dragonfly model](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-2534). No source is being misrepresented as a proof of Venus-runway takeoff.

### (h) Reusable landing gear, crush, and airbags

**ONLINE / high:** Mars Pathfinder airbags and crush/retrocket-assisted descent illustrate **single-mission** energy attenuation, not repeated 735 K Venus service. A 2026 submersible accident report is a useful warning against unvalidated repeated compression structures. **ASSUMPTION / low:** a **35–60 t** landing mass with **3–6 m/s** residual vertical speed implies **~0.16–1.08 MJ** kinetic impact energy; over an illustrative **0.3–1.0 m** crush/stroke, average force can range **~0.16–3.6 MN**, depending on joint endpoints, before lateral loads. ### (i) Venus balloon as pod lifeboat: HAVOC / VeGa

**ONLINE / high for cloud-altitude premise:** VeGa balloons flew ~**46.5 h**; NASA HAVOC investigated inhabited airships at **~50 km** where temperature/pressure are comparatively Earthlike but sulfuric acid aerosols persist. ## Cross-cutting Venus mission closure (not a sizer)

Mass and thrust **must** be evaluated with a shared, conserved inventory. The historical **23.1 t water capacity** must not be combined with the new v1 **35.4 t dry mass**; **v1 total water capacity and wet launch mass are OPEN**, so **no v1 ideal delta-v or round-trip reserve** can be computed without a new, explicit independent assumption. A Venus orbit requires ~7.3 km/s of tangential orbital speed plus an actual drag/gravity/trajectory budget; the craft cannot claim return solely from a mode exhaust speed. At fixed 760 kN, **40 km/s** exhaust requires **15.2 GW ideal jet power**; one cannot have high ve, high thrust and the old 3.4 GW input simultaneously. The 1.6 t 'spare' figure has not survived a mission-state water audit.

Must preserve physically separate but coupled accounts for: **(1)** water as reaction mass, **(2)** protected crew thermal/life-support water, **(3)** hot-side heat rejection/active cooler power, **(4)** dry pressure shell/coolant mechanisms, **(5)** custody capsule/return disposition. Cold-water heat capacity cannot be assigned to both propulsion and continuous 18 h refrigeration. Heat rejected into 735 K atmosphere requires a hotter rejection interface, not a 300 K radiator.

## Top five ranked by uncertainty removal for Venus surface–orbit return

1. **Close altitude-/phase-resolved ascent delta-v and water ledger with the actual thrust/ve modes.** This tests whether reusable surface-to-orbit return fits **any** water budget and reveals the required speed/time/lift. Largest feasibility discriminator; neither 1.6 t spare nor 14.5 km/s exhaust is earned.
2. **Resolve the GW source-to-jet interface and operating mode.** Actual electrical/direct thermal extraction, total efficiency, high-temperature thruster/nozzle mass and power debit; 3.4 GW input already fails the ideal 760 kN/9 km/s bound. First-order mass/heat constraint.
3. **Bound hot-side heat leakage and active cooling/storage for a crewed 18 h mission.** Pressure-vessel penetrations, usable cold water, k(T,p), cooler COP, rejector to hotter-than-ambient, failure response. Tests both 18 h shuttle and 3–6 h pod survival.
4. **Determine ascent aerodynamics and high-temperature ground interface.** Quantify biconic lift/drag, deployable surface envelope, run distance, contact traction, landing loads, impingement, viable launch sites. Does rolling takeoff *actually* save water, or add unworkable heat/structure?
5. **Size the pressure sphere + rover/cabin architecture against repeated 92-bar cycling.** Structural shell, penetrations, mounts, thermal kit, cabin mass and available occupied volume; fundamental packaging/crew-mass constraint, but second to orbital energy.

These are **research priorities**, not tasks authorized for implementation. None is credited with mass or water savings.
