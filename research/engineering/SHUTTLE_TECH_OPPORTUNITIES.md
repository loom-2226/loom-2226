## Executive finding

The new rotor-first Venus/Titan doctrine reduces one atmospheric-lift water-demand hypothesis but does not resolve the orbit return. The pressure sphere and hot mechanisms are plausible technology *families*; no available evidence qualifies the combined Venus surface–orbit round trip. The first-order unknowns are **ascent energy/water, thrust power source, exhaust speed at useful thrust, and thermal protection during surface residence**. The proposed 760 kN water thruster at 9 km/s has **3.42 GW ideal kinetic jet power**; the v1 **3.4 GW was ideal jet power**, not claimed net electrical input. Under an assumed 30–70% electrical-to-jet efficiency, an electrical implementation requires **~4.9–11.4 GW**, potentially above the historical 7.7 GW torch-output entry (itself not a certified shuttle input). The v1 retained **23.1 t water tank capacity as an explicit ASSUMPTION**, copied unchanged from a LOST-sizer historical output; the two records must still remain provenance-distinct. Venus low circular-orbit speed is ~7.3 km/s, not an ascent budget: trajectory, aerodynamic lift, gravity/drag losses and separately defined engine modes must be solved. The power check is **DERIVED CONDITIONAL** from unqualified v1 values, not rejection of the mission objective.

## Q3: current evidence and 2226 opportunity ranges

### (a) Venus hardware: Venera, VeGa, LLISSE, HOTTech, SiC/GaN, hot actuators and seals

**ONLINE / high:** Venus near-surface **~89–94 bar, 735–737 K**, dense ~65 kg/m³ CO2. Soviet Venera 13 transmitted **127 min**, Venera 14 **57 min**; VeGa 1/2 balloons returned ~**46.5 h** at cloud altitude while surface landers survived **~56–57 min**. NASA Glenn SiC circuits survived **521 h** in simulated Venus gas and later **60 days** in simulated conditions; this is electronics, **not a crewed lander**. HOTTech targets **500°C for ≥60 days**, and Honeybee tested extreme-temperature gearing at **486°C**; seals, bearings, bulkhead feedthroughs, wheels and repeated-duty qualification remain integration problems. GaN transistor work reports **11 days** in 460°C/94 bar Venus-like gas; SiC and GaN are not whole-spacecraft proof.

**ONLINE range:** historical lander service **~0.95–2.12 h**; tested hot circuit endurance **~521–1,440 h** in differing conditions; hot environment **~89–94 bar / 735–737 K**. **PROJECTION / medium-low:** hot-side sensors/actuators can move out of chilled cabin, but no numeric mass saving until inventories are partitioned. **v1 verdict:** *supports* distributed hot controls; *leaves open* 18 h cold-water stay, seals, cycle life, crew-egress and return. Sources: [NASA Venus facts](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html), [Venus mission compilation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6839549/), [NASA SiC](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/science-enabling-technology/technology-highlights/integrated-circuits-to-enable-exploration-of-the-harshest-environments-in-solar-system/), [HOTTech](https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/pesto/hottech/), [gearhead](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/8984), [GaN experiment](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0186976).

### (b) External-pressure crew sphere: titanium, ceramic, glass

**ONLINE / high:** Alvin's certified human submersible has a titanium **2.1 m inside-diameter sphere**, **7.4 cm wall**, **4.8 m³ personnel volume**, operated to **6,500 m** depth; entire Alvin mass **~20.4 t** must **not** be assigned to the sphere. Deep-ocean pressure hull safety investigations underscore buckling/imperfections, repeated external-pressure loads, windows and interfaces; neither glass nor ceramic is established as a reusable, penetrated, crew-rated shuttle sphere.

**PROJECTION / low screening only:** For **2.8 m OD Ti** and **~9 MPa external differential**, shell **~0.03–0.07 m** and corresponding ideal geometric Ti shell **~3.2–7.6 t** using 4,430 kg/m³. This is *not* a pressure/buckling-rated thickness or mass (no welds, openings, load paths, safety factor, heat-creep or hot fatigue). **OPEN:** cabin mass (the lost sizer's value), rover/cabin mating structure, hot pressure cycling, pressure seals and window scheme. **v1 verdict:** *supports* sphere topology, *leaves open* mass and 4-person crew practicality. Sources: [WHOI Alvin specifications](https://ndsf.whoi.edu/alvin/specifications/), [WHOI FAQ](https://www.whoi.edu/what-we-do/explore/underwater-vehicles/hov-alvin/faqs/), [TSB pressure-hull investigation](https://tsb-bst.gc.ca/eng/medias-media/fiches-facts/m23a0169/m23a0169-20260617-03.html). Screening equations: shell mass ~4πr²tρ; external-pressure buckling **cannot** be certified by this relation.

### (c) Insulation in ~9 MPa supercritical CO2 at 735 K

**ONLINE / high:** NASA ALIVE/COMPASS insulated the **interior of the pressure vessel with aerogel** and modeled ~**735 K outside, 300 K inside**, plus heat leakage through supports, wiring and windows. Vacuum multilayer insulation is **not** a directly transferrable exterior solution in dense supercritical gas: gas conduction and ingress defeat vacuum assumptions unless a sealed evacuated interspace is actually supplied.

**ASSUMPTION / low sensitivity only:** internal aerogel/porous insulation *effective* conductivity **0.02–0.10 W/(m·K)** and thickness **0.05–0.15 m** (not a Venus-tested material curve); **2.8 m OD** sphere area ~**24.6 m²** implies ideal conduction ~**1.4–20.9 kW** across 435 K depending on joint choices, **before** penetrations, atmosphere convection, heat stores, and inside equipment. **v1 verdict:** *leaves open* thermal kit mass and 18 h/3–6 h claims; conservative thermal leakage is potentially many kW, not automatically a tiny ice-pack load. Source: [NASA ALIVE COMPASS Final Report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160011272). Numerical sensitivity is explicitly a model input, **not** attributed to NASA.

### (d) Active refrigeration on Venus: Stirling

**ONLINE / medium-high:** ALIVE studied duplex Stirling with **~300 W cooling** while consuming **~1,500 W electrical for the cooler**, using a high-temperature heat engine/burner and hot-side rejection. The study used **~35% of Carnot** cooler performance as an assumption; not a demonstrated 2026 crew module. At 300 K cold / 735 K hot, ideal Carnot refrigerator COP **0.69**. 

**PROJECTION / low-to-medium screening:** real COP **~0.07–0.24** for **10–35% of Carnot** assumed, equivalent to **~4.1–14.5 kW electrical input per kW lifted**, plus rejected heat of **~5.1–15.5 kW per kW lifted** at hot end. Do not count Stirling as heat elimination. **v1 verdict:** *challenges* cold-water-only endurance unless the measured leakage, hot-side heat rejection, and stored-energy/water integration close. **OPEN:** cold-water heat capacity and temperature window, cooling duty, refrigeration mass, and true 18 h/3–6 h thermal endurance. Source: [NASA ALIVE](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160011272). This source is a 2012 concept, not flight-qualified hardware.

Sources: [NASA DFD tech project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/93994), [E1 physical-bounds review](LOOM_E1_TORCH_PHYSICAL_BOUNDS_RESEARCH_2026-09-15.md).

As a contingency **comparison only**, an *additional unplanned* 760 kN, 9 km/s belly burn would use 1.6 t in ~19 s. **v1 verdict:** *supports* 3.4 GW **as rounded ideal jet power**; *leaves open* the ~4.9–11.4 GW electrical or equivalent direct-energy supply, high-power hardware mass and surface duty cycle. ### (l) Dense-atmosphere rotors: Venus and Titan primary lift, 60 t / four 6 m diameter screen

**kT DIRECTION / ASSUMPTION:** Venus and Titan use deployable rotors for near-surface flight and controlled landing; the rotor stage hands off to separate torch propulsion **above the useful dense atmosphere**. Rotors **cannot propel to orbit** when the air thins. Moon, Mars and Mercury use small belly thrusters (Mars' thin atmosphere does not qualify it for heavy rotors); Earth launches via spaceport infrastructure and imposes no autonomous shuttle liftoff requirement.

**ONLINE / high:** Momentum theory gives ideal hover power \(P_i=W\sqrt{W/(2\rho A)}\); measured/real rotor *figure of merit* FM is \(P_i/P_{rotor}\), includes induced/profile losses, and is <1. [NASA rotor-figure-of-merit research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19970005490) and [NASA NDARC](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160012474) support the method. Mi-26 is a **56 t maximum takeoff mass, 32 m diameter** single-main-rotor Earth helicopter with two ~8.4 MW engines, **not evidence of a 6 m / 15 t per-rotor design** ([operator data](https://rosguard.gov.ru/page/index/mi26-tyazhlyj-transportnyj-vertolt)). NASA [Dragonfly](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dragonfly/spacecraft-and-instruments/) is a **~875 kg** Titan-design aircraft with eight **~1.35 m** rotor sets (published mission vehicle dimensions ~3.85 m), whereas [Ingenuity](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/ingenuity-mars-helicopter/) flew at **1.8 kg / 1.2 m coaxial rotors** in very thin Martian air. **DOI verified** [AIAA 2026-2534](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-2534) models Dragonfly and explicitly discusses dense-air induced-velocity limits. **NASA** considered controllable rotary-wing descent on Venus, not an operational reusable 60 t hot rotor ([2016 stopped rotor](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160012563), [Venus rotary-wing decelerator](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060010361)).

**DERIVED CONDITIONAL / high arithmetic, low actual performance:** Four **independent, non-overlapping 6 m-diameter disks** give **113.10 m²** total. For **60,000 kg** and **assumed FM 0.60–0.80**: Venus (**g 8.87 m/s², rho 65 kg/m³**) ideal **3.20 MW**, rotor shaft/aerodynamic **4.00–5.34 MW**; Titan (**g 1.352, rho 5.3**) ideal **0.667 MW**, **0.834–1.112 MW**; Earth (**g 9.81, rho 1.225**) ideal **27.13 MW**, **33.91–45.21 MW**. At **FM 0.70** the three values are **4.57, 0.95, 38.75 MW**, respectively. Claude's **4.6 MW Venus** and **39 MW Earth** agree; Claude's **0.5 MW Titan fails the theoretical 0.667 MW minimum** for these inputs. FM ranges are *ASSUMPTION*, not transferred measured efficiencies, and this excludes electric/thermal generator, gearbox, powerbus and motor losses. On Earth, disk loading and blade stall may defeat FM 0.7 even if the arithmetic power looks reasonable; Earth autonomous liftoff is not required.

**PACKAGING / PROJECTION LOW:** A 6 m deployed rotor exceeds the **4.3 m bay diameter**; four hubs and blades must fold, rotate or telescope into the **12.8 m** stowed length, including safe boom/root deployment, tip clearance, crew pressure-pod and docking. NASA [OpenVSP folding blade geometry](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/openvsp-propellers/) demonstrates modeling, not certified hardware. A **1–10 t total rotor/blade/boom/hub/hinge package** is an intentionally broad **ASSUMPTION sensitivity**, **not** an online-derived weight, mass saving, or automatically additive mass: current v1 ledger inclusion is **OPEN**. Real Venus rotor blade metal/ceramic-composite strength, creep, corrosion, aerodynamic shape, high-pressure CO2 downwash, shaft seal/bearing life, and cyclic folding losses are **OPEN**. **Verdict:** *strongly supports* rotor-first research and dramatically reduces surface water demand *if* lift and mass close; **no credited water savings** before a full mission ledger.

### (m) Fairey Rotodyne-type tip jets: hot CO2 as externally ingested working fluid

**ONLINE / high for historical method:** Fairey Rotodyne flew with tip-jet driven lift rotors and switched to autorotation in forward flight; tip burners used **compressed, externally supplied air**, not shaft motors at the rotor. Its **noise**, jet relight, compressor and hot rotating tip hardware were real engineering problems. The historical Rotodyne **did still require an engine-driven compressor and propulsors**. Sources: [Vertical Flight Society](https://legacy.vtol.org/vstol/VSTOLWheel/FaireyRotodyne.htm), [NASA historical rotorcraft review](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930008694).

**PROJECTION / low:** On Venus, **ingest high-pressure ambient CO2**, compress/route through blades, **add fusion reactor heat** and discharge via nozzles at tips. This exchanges momentum with **external atmospheric mass** and can yield **zero tank-water consumption during aerodynamic lift**, but **NOT zero power, cooling, compressor work, or reactor propellant use**. Heat must be added **above the 735 K ambient thermal state**, then expanded with enough nozzle pressure ratio and useful tangential thrust; direct ambient heat with no temperature/pressure differential cannot power a continuous rotor. A thermally driven compressor/turbomachinery avoids *rotor-shaft electrical motors* but **not** compressors, rotary gas unions, sealed hot passages, hub/bearing loads or controls. Gas flow/exergy, effective tip jet thrust, exhaust temperature, boundary mixing and sonic-speed limits in dense CO2 are **OPEN**.

**ASSUMPTION / very low screening:** If **10–35%** of delivered source thermal power becomes **useful rotor shaft-equivalent aerodynamic power** (a scenario, **not measured Rotodyne efficiency**), the **4.00–5.34 MW** Venus 60 t rotor result implies **~11–54 MW reactor thermal input**, plus separate auxiliaries/hot-side rejection. No mass or endurance benefit is credited. **v1 verdict:** *supports* waterless Venus surface hovering in principle, *leaves open* thermal-to-tip-jet efficiency, rotor mass, power tapping, 735 K bearings, blade passages, and safe crew ramp/downwash. **Do not** assume the 7.7 GW historical propulsion entry implies an extracted 11–54 MW thermal interface.

### (n) Electric motor driven rotor alternative: hot machine vs cooled motor

**ONLINE / high:** NASA's extreme-environments work reports a **small switched-reluctance / no-permanent-magnet Venus prototype** cycled around **460°C and ~90 bar** for about **2 hours**; it was not a multi-MW aircraft motor. NASA [Venus actuator project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/92290) and [HOTTech II durable-actuator project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/116074) target **instrument/robotic actuation**, not crewed rotor drives. Commercial [SmCo operating limits](https://www.duramag.com/samarium-cobalt-magnets-smco/available-samarium-cobalt-magnet-grades/) are often **300–350°C**, much below Venus **~462°C**, even when Curie temperatures are higher; **Curie temperature is not a safe motor operating rating**.

**PROJECTION / low:** Magnet-free induction/switched-reluctance motors and high-temperature windings/ceramic bearings avoid conventional magnet demagnetization, but their **MW continuous torque, insulation, bearings, power electronics and fatigue at Venus surface conditions are OPEN**. Cooled conventional motors/possibly superconducting magnetic systems exchange a hot-machine problem for cryocooling, insulation and heat-rejection problems. **Superconductivity at 735 K ambient is not asserted**, and no levitation or refrigeration mass credit is allowed.

**ASSUMPTION / low sensitivity:** If **0.85–0.96** is eventual total electrical-to-rotor mechanical efficiency, **4.00–5.34 MW rotor shaft** demands **~4.17–6.28 MW electric**, with **~0.17–0.94 MW electrical conversion/motor heat** that must be conducted/rejected at the appropriate hot/cold interface. This is not an established 2226 motor performance forecast. **OPEN:** installed motor/gear/bus/radiator/cooler mass (no evidence-backed tonnes), leak into the cold crew volume, hot rotating seals, field/service cycle. **v1 verdict:** electric rotors are an engineering alternative, **not** a proven compact or low-thermal-burden option compared with tip jets.

### (o) Fallback folding wings: Venus lift and required takeoff strip

**ONLINE / medium:** Dense Venus air is hospitable to aerodynamic lift, and researchers have studied [Venus fixed-wing drones](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2020.11.055), but not a reusable 60 t Venus runway shuttle. The earlier [2026 Dragonfly DOI](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-2534) concerns **Titan rotorcraft flight**, not Venus wings.

**DERIVED CONDITIONAL / high arithmetic:** At Venus **rho ~65 kg/m³**, **60 t** and **g 8.87**, \(S=W/(0.5\rho V^2 C_L)\). For **30–40 m/s** and **CL 0.5–1.5** (**ASSUMPTION**) the entire area interval is **~6.8–36.4 m²**. Claude's **~7–12 m²** is attainable only toward **higher airspeed and/or unusually high effective CL**; it is **not** a general area requirement at 30 m/s. If horizontal net acceleration is **2–6 m/s²** (ASSUMPTION, with no solved propulsion/rolling drag), kinematic acceleration run **v²/(2a) ~75–400 m** before steering margins, rotation, abort/braking or terrain clearance; no general runway availability is canon. **OPEN:** folding 735 K surfaces inside the bay, support mass, actuation, thermal creep, area remaining exposed while stowed, wheel contact, surface roughness and actual low-speed CL/CD. **v1 verdict:** keep **wing-assisted rolling liftoff only if rotor path fails**; no wing tonne or water savings credited.
