# Research Dossier — Sara Imari Walker, LOOM Border-Crosser v1.0

**Status:** annotated reference corpus; non-canon.  
**Access date:** 7 September 2026.

Sara Imari Walker is attached to both reviewer squads because her program sits exactly at the seam LOOM cares about: rigorous measurement and causal analysis on one side; genuinely radical questions about information, emergence, objecthood and life on the other.

## Core works

### 1. Walker, S.I. & Davies, P.C.W. — _The Algorithmic Origins of Life_
- Journal of the Royal Society Interface 10 (2013); arXiv:1207.4803.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4803
- Proposes that the transition from non-life to life may be understood as a change in causal architecture in which information gains direct, context-dependent causal efficacy over the matter in which it is instantiated.
- Important detail: the proposal does not treat information as magic. The challenge is to identify measurable changes in causal organization, including top-down influence and distributed control.
- **LOOM use:** X2 and S5 question generation. It gives us a much more disciplined version of the vague question "can information do things?" The LOOM version becomes: what measurable intervention/causal criterion distinguishes a merely descriptive state variable from an informational organization that changes lower-level dynamics?
- **Not evidence for:** M2, relational control, consciousness, metric engineering, or a new fundamental force.

### 2. Walker, S.I. — _Top-Down Causation and the Rise of Information in the Emergence of Life_
- Information 5 (2014), 424–439. DOI:10.3390/info5030424.
- https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/5/3/424
- Reviews and extends the idea that life's origin can be framed as a transition in information flow and causal structure.
- Discusses top-down causation, control, hierarchy, feedback, and candidate information-flow measures.
- **LOOM use:** X2/S5 methodology. If LOOM ever uses language like "information controls relational structure," we should demand intervention-level or transfer-level evidence analogous in spirit to this program rather than treating semantic information as a physical source term by fiat.

### 3. Walker, S.I. & Davies, P.C.W. — _The "Hard Problem" of Life_
- arXiv:1606.07184 (2016).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07184
- Frames the central puzzle of life as explaining how information can become a causal agent and argues that known physical principles may be insufficient to explain the phenomenon in full.
- **LOOM use:** X2 wild-card branch. The important value is the form of the problem: separate a descriptive information vocabulary from a claim of genuine causal efficacy and ask what new principle, if any, is actually required.
- **Hazard:** HIGH. The paper's suggestion that new principles may be necessary is not evidence that any specific new principle exists.

### 4. Sharma, A., Czégel, D., Lachmann, M., Kempes, C.P., Walker, S.I. & Cronin, L. — _Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution_
- Nature 622, 321–328 (2023). DOI:10.1038/s41586-023-06600-9.
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06600-9
- Defines objects partly through possible formation histories and combines assembly index with copy number to quantify signatures of selection.
- The theory explicitly tries to account for historical contingency and construction pathways in physical descriptions of complex objects.
- **LOOM use:** X3/F3 xenoarchaeology and S4 identity questions. It suggests a useful distinction between an object's present-state description and the causal/history structure required to produce it.
- Potential xeno application: characterize artifacts by construction-depth, repeated motifs, copy structure and inferred selection without immediately assigning purpose or agency.
- **Not evidence for:** alien origin, consciousness, or a universal ontology of objects.

### 5. Walker, S.I. — _Metrology of Complexity and Implications for the Study of the Emergence of Life_
- arXiv:2602.18203 (2026).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18203
- Argues that progress on life's emergence requires standardized measurement frameworks capable of cross-comparing complexity across experiments and environments; emphasizes assembly-theory-derived molecular complexity and explicit metrology.
- **LOOM use:** F3/X3 and broader research governance. This is highly aligned with our insistence that "strange" phenomena become useful only when converted into measurements, standards and discriminating tests.
- Potential TMP/Reef use: create a metrology ladder before creating a story about what an artifact "means."

## Walker-derived LOOM research questions

1. Can causal efficacy be operationalized by interventions rather than semantic description?
2. What distinguishes information encoded in a state from information that changes the allowed dynamics of that state?
3. Can a transition in causal architecture be detected before its mechanism is understood?
4. Does identity of a complex object depend only on present state, or also on constrained formation history?
5. Can alien technological selection be detected via complexity/copy/history measures without assuming biology or intentional design?
6. What metrology would let different LOOM experiments compare "complexity," "organization" or "control" without using those words impressionistically?

## Program role

### Adults With Red Pens
Walker forces:
- measurable definitions;
- intervention/causal criteria;
- complexity metrology;
- separation of history-sensitive claims from static pattern recognition.

### Respectably Unsupervised
Walker licenses asking, carefully:
- whether information can become a causal organizing variable;
- whether new physical principles might be needed for some emergence problems;
- whether objecthood/history should enter the physical description more deeply than standard state variables suggest.

## Hard boundary

Walker is a **bridge**, not a shortcut.

LOOM SHALL NOT write:
> "Walker shows information controls matter, therefore M2 is plausible."

The permissible move is:
> "Walker and collaborators provide a concrete research program for asking when information is causally efficacious in living/complex systems; LOOM can borrow the question structure and measurement discipline while independently testing any M2-adjacent hypothesis."
