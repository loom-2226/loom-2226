# LOOM 2226 — Freaks With Clipboards Ancestral Lineage v1.0

**Formal scope:** historical ancestry for the LOOM Anomalous Phenomenology & High-Strangeness Review Squad.

**Epistemic rule:** this lineage is not a list of people LOOM thinks were right. It is a lineage of **case collection, classification, phenomenology, instrumentation, skeptical control, contested experiments, witness handling, cross-domain interpretation and failure modes** relevant to LOOM's Strangeness Problem.

## 1. Charles Fort — anomaly before explanation

### Relevant works
- **The Book of the Damned** (1919).
- **New Lands** (1923).
- **Lo!** (1931).
- **Wild Talents** (1932).

### Why LOOM cares
Fort's enduring methodological provocation is to retain data that established categories discard too quickly. LOOM inherits the useful part—preserve anomalies and provenance—while rejecting Fortian accumulation as sufficient evidence for any ontology.

### LOOM use
Historical ancestry for the Strangeness Problem, `GHOST` libraries, anomaly registry design, F5/F7.

### Boundary
Catalogued strangeness is not a validated causal class.

Primary text: Project Gutenberg, *The Book of the Damned*: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/22472

## 2. Henry Sidgwick, Eleanor Sidgwick, Edmund Gurney, F. W. H. Myers and the early Society for Psychical Research

### Relevant work
- Society for Psychical Research founded 1882 to investigate disputed mesmeric, psychical and spiritualistic claims.
- Gurney, Myers & Podmore, **Phantasms of the Living** (1886).
- F. W. H. Myers, **Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death** (1903).
- extensive SPR Proceedings on apparitions, telepathy claims, mediumship, hypnosis, fraud controls and witness testimony.

### Why LOOM cares
The useful ancestry is institutional: create a place where extraordinary reports can be documented while fraud, memory, suggestion and alternative explanation are treated as first-class possibilities. The early SPR also demonstrates how quickly investigators can diverge over ontology even while sharing cases.

### LOOM use
TAAM/TAAC history, witness methodology, Patternist institutional history, Freaks governance.

Reference: Society for Psychical Research historical overview: https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/society-psychical-research

## 4. J. B. Rhine + Louisa Rhine — laboratory and spontaneous-case parapsychology

### Relevant work
- J. B. Rhine, **Extra-Sensory Perception** (1934).
- card-guessing/ESP laboratory programs at Duke.
- Louisa Rhine's large spontaneous-case collections and classifications.

### Why LOOM cares
The Rhine program is useful as both ancestry and caution: operationalizing extraordinary claims makes statistical testing possible, while experimental design, sensory leakage, replication, optional stopping and publication bias become decisive.

### LOOM use
C-methodology history; X2 negative/positive controls; F5 sociology of controversial science.

### Boundary
Historical statistical claims do not establish psi as a LOOM physical input.

## 5. J. Allen Hynek — classification, strangeness and probability

### Relevant work
- **The UFO Experience: A Scientific Inquiry** (1972).
- Close Encounter taxonomy.
- explicit **Strangeness Rating** and **Probability Rating** dimensions.
- Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS).

### Why LOOM cares
Hynek is one of the strongest direct ancestors of the existing LOOM anomaly design. He separated how strange a report is from how credible/well-supported it is. That is extremely close to LOOM's insistence that weirdness and evidence strength are different axes.

### LOOM use
Strangeness Problem; future H/X/Window/Flap provenance; Anomalous Phenomena skill; report triage.

### Boundary
A high-strangeness/high-probability report remains an unidentified report, not proof of extraterrestrial or relational cause.

References:
- CUFOS classification overview: https://cufos.org/types-of-ufos/classification-systems/
- CUFOS-hosted *The UFO Experience*: https://cufos.org/PDFs/books/The_UFO_Experience.pdf

## 10. Ian Stevenson — longitudinal, coded anomalous-memory case corpus

### Relevant work
- **Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation** (1966/1974 editions).
- **Children Who Remember Previous Lives** (1987; rev. 2001).
- **Reincarnation and Biology** (1997).
- decades of field investigation later continued by UVA Division of Perceptual Studies.

### Why LOOM cares
Whatever one concludes about reincarnation, Stevenson created a unusually large longitudinal case corpus with structured variables, attempts to obtain statements before verification, cultural comparisons and explicit consideration of fraud, memory error, cryptomnesia and alternative hypotheses. UVA reports >2,500 cases and >200 coded variables for most.

### LOOM use
TAAM design methodology; memory provenance; case-database schema; F5 institutional history.

### Boundary
The existence of a coded case corpus does not establish reincarnation. Stevenson himself considered multiple competing interpretations.

References:
- UVA DOPS research overview: https://med.virginia.edu/perceptual-studies/our-research/children-who-report-memories-of-previous-lives/
- UVA warning against hypnotic regression: https://med.virginia.edu/perceptual-studies/resources/concerns-about-hypnotic-regression/

## 13. Robert G. Jahn + Brenda Dunne / PEAR — mind-machine anomaly experiments

### Relevant work
- Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research laboratory, 1979–2007.
- Jahn & Dunne, **Margins of Reality** (1987).
- large random-event-generator/random-mechanical-cascade datasets.
- remote-perception experiments.

### Why LOOM cares
PEAR is valuable both as experimental ancestry and controversy: it tried to put small anomalous mind/machine effects into controlled quantitative form and reported small statistical deviations, while replication, controls, effect sizes and interpretation remained disputed.

### LOOM use
X2; C methodology; negative-control design; F5 sociology.

### Boundary
PEAR claims do not establish consciousness control of physical systems and cannot be used to calibrate M2.

References:

## 21. Phenomenological lineages the main roster inherits

- Vallée inherits and revises Fort/Hynek while resisting one-cause UFO ontology.

- Cardeña inherits experimental/anomalous-experience psychology.

- Radin inherits Rhine/experimental psi and therefore also inherits its replication/statistics burden.
- Strand directly continues the instrumented recurrent-anomaly tradition.
- Lazar is a **case/witness node inside the roster**, not ancestry/method authority: a durable test case for provenance, credential disputes, technical claim analysis and myth formation.

## 22. What Freaks ancestry contributes to LOOM

This lineage earns a place because LOOM already has a **Strangeness Problem**. Its best contribution is not 'paranormal explanations'. It is a methodology for handling ugly data:

1. preserve raw observation and chain of custody;
2. separate experience from inferred ontology;
3. classify before explaining;
4. preserve mundane explanations and fraud controls;
5. weight independent sensors and independent witnesses;
6. model selection/reporting effects;
7. retain residual cases without treating residual as a causal class;
8. pass anything genuinely discriminating to the Respectably Unsupervised for model generation and then to the Adults With Red Pens for attempted murder.

That sequence is the real Freaks inheritance.
