# LOOM 2226 — Freaks With Clipboards Current-Roster Dossier v1.0

**Formal name:** LOOM Anomalous Phenomenology & High-Strangeness Review Squad  
**Status:** research ancestry / methods / question generation; no source here calibrates LOOM physics.

## Operating premise

This squad is deliberately mixed. 'Rigor' means different things across the roster:
- rigorous conventional laboratory science applied near an anomalous topic;
- disciplined archival/humanities study of anomalous belief and experience;
- systematic case classification and provenance work;
- experimental work making controversial claims that must carry replication/statistical counterweights;
- skeptical/anomalistic psychology;
- persistent field instrumentation;
- or, for one explicitly tagged seat, a contested high-impact primary witness whose claims are themselves part of the provenance problem.

Membership is not equivalence.

# 10. Bob Lazar — Contested Primary-Witness Seat

## Why he is on the current roster at all
The user explicitly wants the 'Area 51 guy' represented. Lazar is therefore retained as a **case/witness seat**, not a scientific-review seat.

## Core claim corpus
Beginning publicly in 1989, Lazar claimed that he worked as a physicist at a secret S-4 facility near Area 51 on reverse engineering of nonhuman craft and described a propulsion narrative involving element 115.

## Why LOOM cares
Lazar is an unusually durable case for studying:
- primary-witness claims and changing retellings;
- credential verification and missing/disputed records;
- classified-program plausibility versus extraordinary extrapolation;
- technical claims that can be separated into contemporaneously testable, later-testable and non-falsifiable pieces;
- journalist/intermediary effects;
- mythology and cultural persistence;
- how a contested witness can become part of a community's prior model of all later evidence.

## Critical counterweight
Public summaries note major credential disputes, including lack of records from institutions he said he attended and disputes over his claimed Los Alamos role. These disputes are themselves part of the provenance problem and must travel with any analysis.

## LOOM use
P5; F5/F7; witness-provenance schema; myth-formation models.

## Does not establish
Lazar's claims are not scientific evidence for alien craft, element-115 propulsion, Area 51 reverse engineering, M1/M2 or any LOOM technology.

Reference for the historical/cultural claim and credential controversy: National Geographic, **How aliens and UFOs became part of Area 51 lore** (2026).

# Squad-level research questions inherited by LOOM

1. Can we design a case schema where **strangeness, reliability, independence and explanatory residual** are separate variables?
2. What evidence counts as genuinely independent in a world full of shared media, model contamination and witness discussion?
3. How do we preregister anomaly analysis so a fascinating residual is not created by post-hoc selection?
4. When does multi-sensor convergence materially increase confidence versus merely share one hidden environmental/systematic cause?
5. How should unusual conscious phenomenology be coded without either pathologizing or literalizing it?
6. How do we retain high-impact contested witnesses without allowing charisma/notoriety to dominate the evidence graph?
7. Can a `RESIDUAL` state be first-class—neither `EXPLAINED` nor `EXOTIC`?
8. What exact conditions cause a Freaks observation to earn handoff to the Unsupervised and then the Red Pens?

# Epistemic conclusion

The Freaks With Clipboards are not a third theory of reality.

They are LOOM's permanent reminder that **before the theory fight, somebody has to preserve the ugly evidence object accurately enough that there is still something real to fight about.**
