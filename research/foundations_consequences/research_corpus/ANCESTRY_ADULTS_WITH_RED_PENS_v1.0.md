# LOOM 2226 — Adults With Red Pens Ancestral Lineage v1.0

**Scope:** historical scientific/mathematical ancestry for Squad A. This is a curated, current-LOOM-scope lineage rather than a history of physics. Inclusion means a result, method, formal language, failure mode or constraint materially informs LOOM Foundations & Consequences.

## 1. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz — relational space and time

### Relevant work
- **Leibniz–Clarke Correspondence** (1715–1716), especially Leibniz's third through fifth papers.

### Why LOOM cares
Leibniz's view of space as an order of coexistences and time as an order of successions is the deepest historical ancestor in this corpus for treating spatial/temporal structure relationally rather than as a fixed container.

### LOOM use
S1 pregeometry ancestry; S3B time; X1 reconstruction; philosophical provenance for relational topography.

### Boundary
Leibnizian relationalism is metaphysical ancestry, not modern quantum gravity and not evidence for M1/M2.

Reference: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Leibniz's Philosophy of Physics*: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/leibniz-physics/

## 2. Ernst Mach — critique of absolute motion

### Relevant work
- **The Science of Mechanics** (1883; later editions/translations), especially critique of Newtonian absolute space and inertia.

### Why LOOM cares
Mach provides historical scientific ancestry for asking whether inertial/geometric structure is determined relationally by physical content rather than imposed as an unobservable background.

### LOOM use
S1/S3B historical context; anti-background assumptions.

### Boundary
'Mach's principle' has multiple formulations; LOOM should not claim a single theorem or imply GR uniquely realizes Mach's philosophy.

## 3. Bernhard Riemann — geometry as physical possibility rather than fixed Euclidean stage

### Relevant work
- **On the Hypotheses Which Lie at the Foundations of Geometry** (1854 lecture; published 1868).

### Why LOOM cares
Riemann is foundational ancestry for treating geometry as a variable mathematical structure whose metric properties can be physical rather than immutable background facts.

### LOOM use
S1/S2 geometry reconstruction; metric versus relational geometry vocabulary.

## 4. Albert Einstein — dynamical spacetime and covariance

### Relevant work
- General relativity (1915–1916); later discussions of geometry, fields and general covariance.

### Why LOOM cares
GR makes spacetime geometry dynamical and matter-coupled. It is LOOM's non-negotiable infrared matching target wherever ordinary spacetime is recovered.

### Boundary
General covariance alone is not relational topography, pregeometry, or an M1 mechanism.

## 5. Emmy Noether — symmetry and conservation

### Relevant work
- **Invariant Variation Problems** (1918), Noether's first and second theorems.

### Why LOOM cares
The program's insistence that speculative transport cannot create free energy/momentum ultimately belongs to the symmetry/conservation tradition Noether formalized. At pregeometric levels the hard question becomes what symmetry and conservation mean before ordinary spacetime has been reconstructed.

### LOOM use
S1 parent action; S5 resource accounting; Paper I conservation/Ward-identity ancestry.

### Boundary
Noether's theorem does not supply the required LOOM symmetry or reservoirs; those must be specified by the model.
