# LOOM 2226 — Foundations & Consequences Program Charter v0.1

**Status:** EXPLORATORY RESEARCH PROGRAM — NON-CANON — NON-RUNTIME  
**Program parent:** LOOM Research & Speculative Development Portfolio  
**Date:** 7 September 2026

## 1. Mission

LOOM now contains a coupled scientific, speculative, civilizational and operational body of work. The purpose of this program is to turn that body into a controlled portfolio rather than an unbounded set of rabbit holes.

The program asks four different questions at once:

1. **Empirical / Mathematical:** what can be derived, measured, falsified or formally constrained?
2. **Speculative / Synthetic:** what is worth constructing and exploring rigorously even when not established?
3. **Civilizational / Fictional:** what happens when humans, synthetics and institutions live with those possibilities?
4. **Adversarial / Wild:** what alternative assumptions, maverick questions, failures and negative controls expose hidden weaknesses?

These are co-equal discovery engines with different burdens of proof.

## 2. Claim discipline

The epistemic stack remains inherited from the Relational Foundations program:

- **E** — established physics or mathematics;
- **A** — analogy / mathematical ancestry;
- **P** — fictional postulate;
- **C** — constitutive closure / canon calibration;
- **D** — derived result;
- **O** — open problem.

A work item may move between lanes, but a claim may not silently change epistemic class.

The operational rule is:

> We can go anywhere. We must know what kind of claim we are making while we are there.

## 3. Program outcomes

A branch is valuable if it produces any of the following:

- **DERIVATION** — removes or reduces a fictional permission by deriving a deeper parent structure;
- **CONSTRAINT** — sharpens what LOOM physics, engineering or worldbuilding must satisfy;
- **FALSIFICATION** — kills an attractive but unsupported mechanism or interpretation;
- **GENERATIVITY** — creates scientifically honest but fictionally powerful unresolved structure;
- **INSTRUMENTATION** — improves what can be measured, compared or falsified next;
- **HISTORICAL VALUE** — a failed idea remains useful as an in-world intellectual `GHOST`.

## 4. Scoring criteria

Each program branch and work item is scored 0–5 on:

- **Scientific leverage** — ability to derive, falsify, constrain or improve a model;
- **Speculative reach** — ability to open genuinely new conceptual territory;
- **Canon leverage** — ability to clarify an existing LOOM gap without reverse-engineering canon;
- **World yield** — ability to generate institutions, conflict, history, belief or culture;
- **Play yield** — likelihood of producing player-facing consequences;
- **Rabbit-hole joy** — whether the idea is intrinsically worth following.

A separate **Epistemic Hazard** rating (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `EXTREME`) records how easily a branch could be mistaken for established support.

High hazard is not a veto. It requires stronger labeling and stricter promotion gates.

## 5. Portfolio states

- **SEED** — preserve the idea.
- **PROBE** — short exploratory pass justified.
- **RABBIT_HOLE** — enough signal for sustained investigation.
- **PROGRAM_CANDIDATE** — could materially affect LOOM research/world architecture.
- **QUALIFICATION** — formal scientific testing or structured validation underway.
- **PROMOTION_CANDIDATE** — could alter research architecture or canon if independently earned.
- **RETIRED** — currently dead or low-value.
- **GHOST** — disproved/abandoned as explanation but retained for intellectual history, fiction or negative-control value.

## 6. WIP rule

The program SHALL NOT activate every interesting branch simultaneously.

Default program increment allocation:

- ~70% disciplined qualification / validation work;
- ~20% adjacent exploration and framework comparison;
- ~10% wild-card work selected primarily for conceptual fertility.

Default science WIP: one execution branch plus at most two discovery branches.

Default fiction/speculation WIP: no more than three active design branches unless one is explicitly time-boxed.

## 7. Science–speculation–fiction traffic rules

Allowed:

- fiction -> scientific question;
- speculation -> mathematical candidate;
- science -> fiction constraint;
- failed science -> historical/worldbuilding material;
- anomalous fiction requirement -> red-team question;
- maverick idea -> hypothesis or negative control.

Forbidden:

- fictional desirability -> scientific evidence;
- canon compatibility -> preselection of foundations mathematics;
- analogy -> calibration;
- source prestige -> promotion;
- a successful toy model -> proof of M1/M2.
