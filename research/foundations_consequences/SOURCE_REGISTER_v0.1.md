# LOOM 2226 — Foundations & Consequences Source Register v0.1

This register records why external work is relevant. Inclusion means **reference ancestry, question generation, methodological comparison or negative-control value**. It does not imply endorsement or evidentiary support for M1/M2.

## Branch A — scientific/foundational reviewers

### Daniele Oriti
- **Relevant work:** Group Field Theory / tensorial GFT; spacetime emergence; condensate cosmology; relational localization; work with Vincent Lam on laws without fundamental spacetime.
- **Use in LOOM:** S1/S2 geometrogenesis and continuum ancestry; X1 reconstruction; scientific wording around non-spatiotemporal foundations.
- **Why:** Oriti's work treats spacetime emergence as a multi-step reconstruction problem rather than a single slogan, matching LOOM's staged evidence ladder.
- **Representative references:**
  - Oriti, *The universe as a quantum gravity condensate*, Comptes Rendus Physique 18 (2017), arXiv:1612.09521.
  - Oriti, *Levels of spacetime emergence in quantum gravity*, arXiv:1807.04875.
  - Margoni & Oriti, *The emergence of spacetime: what role for functionalism?*, arXiv:2404.11386.
  - Lam & Oriti, *The quantum gravity seeds for laws of nature*, European Journal for Philosophy of Science 14 (2024).

### Bianca Dittrich
- **Relevant work:** discrete quantum geometry, coarse-graining, renormalization, continuum dynamics, tensor-network methods.
- **Use in LOOM:** S2 continuum/universality and D1 anti-answer-smuggling review.
- **Why:** a small discrete model only becomes physically interesting if its large-scale behavior is robust under refinement/coarse-graining.
- **Representative references:** Perimeter Institute research profile and publications on coarse graining / continuum limits in background-independent quantum gravity.

### Sumati Surya
- **Relevant work:** causal set theory; manifoldlikeness; locality and dimension estimators; Lorentz-invariant discreteness.
- **Use in LOOM:** S3A/S3B causality and chronology; locality diagnostics.
- **Why:** causal sets provide a serious model in which partial order is fundamental and spatial geometry is reconstructed rather than assumed.
- **Representative references:** Surya, *The causal set approach to quantum gravity*, Living Reviews in Relativity 22 (2019); Glaser & Surya, *Towards a definition of locality in a manifoldlike causal set* (2013).

### Fotini Markopoulou
- **Relevant work:** Quantum Graphity, geometrogenesis, background-independent dynamics, emergent space with fundamental time.
- **Use in LOOM:** S1 failure ancestry, S3B time architecture, D1.
- **Why:** Quantum Graphity is a close historical analogue to LOOM's risk of obtaining generic highly connected graph phases rather than genuine locality.
- **Representative references:** Konopka, Markopoulou & Smolin, *Quantum Graphity*, arXiv:hep-th/0611197; Markopoulou, *Space does not exist, so time can*, arXiv:0909.1861.

### Lee Smolin
- **Relevant work:** temporal naturalism/relationalism; energetic causal sets; evolving laws.
- **Use in LOOM:** S3B as an adversarial architecture in which causal succession/time is fundamental rather than emergent.
- **Why:** prevents LOOM from treating emergent time as the only intellectually serious option.
- **Representative references:** Smolin, *Temporal naturalism*, Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics 52 (2015); later temporal-relational work including arXiv:1805.12468.

### Emily Adlam
- **Relevant work:** relational observables, structural realism, modality, arrows of time.
- **Use in LOOM:** S4 identity/protected observables; X1 observer/reconstruction questions; S3B.
- **Why:** sharpens the question of what makes two externally different realizations physically the same subsystem rather than merely descriptively equivalent.
- **Representative references:** Adlam, recent work on relational observables and structural realism, including Synthese (2025) and work on observing relational observables.

### Renate Loll
- **Relevant work:** Causal Dynamical Triangulations, Lorentzian path integrals, causal microstructure.
- **Use in LOOM:** S2 and S3A/D1.
- **Why:** CDT is a strong comparison case where causal structure is built into microscopic admissibility rather than hoped to emerge from an unconstrained Euclidean ensemble.
- **Representative references:** Ambjørn, Jurkiewicz & Loll on CDT; Loll's modern reviews of quantum gravity from causal dynamical triangulations.

### Seth Lloyd
- **Relevant work:** quantum information, computation and physical resource accounting.
- **Use in LOOM:** S4/S5/X2; no-copy/no-free-information constraints.
- **Why:** useful for asking what information-theoretic conservation or resource limits must accompany identity/control claims.
- **Representative references:** Lloyd's work on quantum computation and information in physical systems; MIT Center for Quantum Engineering profile.

## Branch B — mavericks / heterodox / speculative references

### Stephen Wolfram
- **Relevant work:** evolving hypergraphs, causal graphs, multiway systems, causal invariance, observer/reconstruction ideas.
- **Use in LOOM:** X1, S3, X4/D1.
- **Why:** supplies concrete computational language separating spatial connectivity from causal update structure and is highly generative for reconstruction questions.
- **Status:** heterodox research ancestry, not consensus evidence.
- **Representative reference:** Wolfram Physics Project technical introduction and appendices on spatial, causal and multiway graphs.

### Julian Barbour
- **Relevant work:** timeless physics, configuration/shape space, Shape Dynamics.
- **Use in LOOM:** S3B/X4.
- **Why:** forces a clean challenge to assumptions that time must be a primitive variable.
- **Representative references:** Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati, *The solution to the problem of time in shape dynamics*, arXiv:1105.0183; Barbour's books and papers on timeless configuration space.

### Donald Hoffman
- **Relevant work:** conscious realism / interface theory; spacetime as reconstructed appearance.
- **Use in LOOM:** X1 question generation only; F1/F4 cultural ancestry.
- **Why:** maximalist version of the substrate-vs-interface distinction can expose hidden assumptions about objecthood and observer-accessible reality.
- **Status:** highly speculative; consciousness ontology must not be imported into LOOM science.

### Bernardo Kastrup
- **Relevant work:** analytic idealism; non-spatial deeper structure; observer/world interpretation.
- **Use in LOOM:** X1/X4 and F1/F4.
- **Why:** useful philosophical stress test for what is meant by physical location/objecthood; not physics evidence.
- **Status:** metaphysical/speculative.

### Federico Faggin
- **Relevant work:** speculative quantum-information panpsychism; intrinsic/private information claims.
- **Use in LOOM:** S4/X2/F2 question generation around copying, private state and continuity.
- **Why:** can provoke useful distinctions between transferable information and constitutive identity even if the consciousness claims are not adopted.
- **Status:** highly speculative.

### Eric Weinstein
- **Relevant work:** Geometric Unity and reconstruction from larger geometric structures.
- **Use in LOOM:** X4/D1, representation/reconstruction analogies.
- **Why:** useful as a radical mathematical-program comparison and as a prompt to ask whether familiar geometry/fields are projections of a larger structure.
- **Status:** controversial/heterodox; no role as M1/M2 evidence.

### Garrett Lisi
- **Relevant work:** E8 unification proposal and subsequent technical criticism.
- **Use in LOOM:** D1/F7 methodology and sociology of radical theory.
- **Why:** a valuable case study in making a bold proposal precise enough to attract concrete mathematical falsification.
- **Representative references:** Lisi, *An Exceptionally Simple Theory of Everything*, arXiv:0711.0770; Distler & Garibaldi, *There is no "Theory of Everything" inside E8*, arXiv:0905.2658.

### Nassim Haramein
- **Relevant work:** speculative gravitational/unification claims including *The Schwarzschild Proton*.
- **Use in LOOM:** X4/D1 negative control and F7 intellectual-history template.
- **Why:** demonstrates the epistemic failure mode of moving too quickly from numerical/structural analogy to sweeping physical mechanism or technological implication.
- **Status:** not used as scientific support.

## Bridge — Sara Imari Walker
- **Relevant work:** assembly theory, information, causation, life's emergence, causal architecture.
- **Use in LOOM:** X2, S5, F2/F3.
- **Why:** unusually useful bridge between serious science and high-speculation questions about when information becomes causally efficacious and how emergence should be measured.
- **Representative references:** Walker & Davies, *The algorithmic origins of life*, Journal of the Royal Society Interface 10 (2013), arXiv:1207.4803; later assembly-theory work with Cronin and collaborators.

## Usage rule

The source register is intentionally pluralist. A source can be scientifically wrong in one domain and still be valuable as:

- a question generator;
- a mathematical analogy;
- an adversarial comparison;
- a historical warning;
- a fictional intellectual ancestor.

Those uses must be declared explicitly.
