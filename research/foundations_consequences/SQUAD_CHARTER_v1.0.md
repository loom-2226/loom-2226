# LOOM 2226 — Reviewer Squads & Intellectual Ancestry Charter v1.0

**Status:** GOVERNING RESEARCH-PROGRAM CONVENTION — NON-CANON — NON-RUNTIME  
**Date:** 7 September 2026

## 1. Why the squads exist

LOOM will preserve two deliberately different external-thinking squads as permanent intellectual infrastructure for the Foundations & Consequences program. The names are an internal LOOM joke, not claims about any individual person's competence or credibility.

The two squads have different jobs:

- one tries to **kill weak scientific claims before we fall in love with them**;
- one tries to **ask sufficiently radical questions before our caution makes LOOM boring**.

The governing maxim remains:

> **Ideas may move freely; evidentiary status may not.**

**Important:** the squad nickname does **not** mean each person is a "crackpot" in the same sense. LOOM records the status of each **claim**, not a blanket judgment of the person.

**What we ask them, conceptually:**
- What assumption would a respectable program never think to question?
- Could space, time, objects, or laws be reconstruction artifacts rather than primitives?
- Is there a more radical representation in which our present variables are projections?

- Which weird question can be made precise enough for the Adults With Red Pens to attack?

**Authority rule:** Squad B primarily contributes `A`, `H`, `M`, `R`, or `F` material: analogy, heterodox hypotheses, metaphysical stimuli, red-team controls, and fiction ancestry. A Squad B result can enter scientific qualification only by satisfying the same independent gates as any other candidate.

## 5. Persistent attribution rule

Every work item in `research/foundations_consequences/` that materially inherits an external idea SHALL include an `intellectual_ancestry` section containing:

- person / collaboration;
- work title;
- year;
- DOI, arXiv identifier, or stable primary URL where available;
- LOOM use: `method`, `ancestry`, `comparison`, `question`, `negative_control`, `fiction_ancestry`;
- epistemic class of the imported claim;
- explicit statement of what the source does **not** establish for LOOM when confusion is plausible.

Attribution persists even if the idea is later retired. A `GHOST` keeps its ancestry.

## 6. Program cross-reference requirement

The squad corpus is not allowed to live only in reviewer notes. It is cross-referenced from:

- Foundations & Consequences program charter;
- workstream map;
- source register;
- portfolio/backlog items when activated;
- reviewer-constellation records;
- repository `docs/` reference index;
- repository `governance/` intellectual-ancestry policy;
- experiment or design notes materially derived from a cited source.

Canon and runtime documents should cite this research corpus only when an idea is actually promoted into those layers. Mere conceptual influence does not become canon by repetition.

## 7. Scientific-credit rule

LOOM SHALL distinguish:

1. **Original source claim** — what the cited author actually proposed or demonstrated.
2. **LOOM interpretation** — our reading of why it matters.
3. **LOOM extension** — a new hypothesis or design move inspired by it.
4. **LOOM result** — what our model/experiment actually found.

These four are never collapsed into one paragraph without labels.

When a LOOM idea is substantially anticipated by existing work, credit the prior work first. "We independently thought of something similar" is not a substitute for citation.

## 8. Research-corpus storage policy

The repository stores **annotated research dossiers, bibliographic metadata, source links, short lawful excerpts where necessary, and LOOM-specific analysis**.

It does not wholesale mirror copyrighted papers. Where an open-access source permits redistribution, the canonical repo record still prefers a stable DOI/arXiv/author-hosted link plus detailed notes unless there is a concrete reproducibility reason to vendor the source.

This keeps the repository usable, attributable and legally clean while preserving enough detail for future LOOM work without having to rediscover the literature.

## 10. Promotion rule

A source can inspire a LOOM hypothesis immediately.

A source cannot change LOOM science or canon merely because it is elegant, famous, weird, mainstream, peer-reviewed, unpopular, or fun.

Promotion still requires the appropriate LOOM evidence gate.

That is the deal: **the Red Pens get to murder things; the Respectably Unsupervised get to keep bringing strange things into the building.**
