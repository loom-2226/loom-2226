## A–F. Option 1 — Gravity steering

**A. Miracles and authority.** The postulated domain applies up to 1.5 times partner gravitational acceleration to its free-falling interior, transfers reaction to the dominant local body, and pays mechanical power `F·v_rel` (three-option brief §1; `EXPERIMENT_2_2026_11_21.md`, Hypothesis A; `bmd1_metric_gradient.py`; `gs_trajectory.py`, commit 78890fd). It can be *classified* as M2 actuator control over M1 geometry (Weave §§15–17), but local-body selection and a bounded local acceleration are additional **constitutive assumptions**. Whether this amounts to a third miracle depends on whether M2 is explicitly extended to cover it; nomenclature alone does not settle physics. II §7.1a's Sun-barycentric ENERGY-B mapping and §7.1b's **Sun** exchange partner do not authorize an Earth/Mars local partner. II §6 certification, §18A tactical propulsion split, §14 torch/metric thermal exclusivity and §7.3 conservation require scoped revision or new certification. II §12.1 only applies if a Loom commit is used, not automatically to continuous gravity steering. I §5.3 permits displacement without equivalent ordinary crew acceleration, not this specific lift law.

**B. Conservation.** On the proposed law the shuttle gains energy `∫F·v_rel dt`; the partner receives opposite momentum, and orbital/angular momentum must also be booked. The 61 t Earth-to-400 km calculation (29 MWe, ~19.4 h, ~2.03 TJ work; 7.7 GW, ~21 min) is a **simplified trajectory** without air drag or Earth rotation, not an independent proof of source/field conservation (`gs_trajectory.py`, 78890fd). At 1 AU the constant-acceleration estimate ~768 m/s/day is not a propagated orbit. Perpendicular force can change direction with little mechanical work; it is not evidence that field formation, holding, losses and body coupling cost nothing. **Pump test:** execute a complete lift, controlled traverse, descent with energy harvest, and field reset, returning shuttle, partner and field to the same states. If net extractable work remains, **kill or revise** under II §7.3. Do not infer a proof from a zero recoil energy of a massive partner.

**C. Scope.** Even a few prototypes imply potential zero-remass orbit insertion and station keeping, and prospective disruption of remass, torch, insurance and strategic sealift. Actual 2226 freight throughput stays negligible with a handful of uncertified craft, but financial expectations may move immediately. The non-canon `GRAVITY_STEERING_SCARCITY_ECONOMY_2026_11_21.md` (86fa8ec) distinguishes demonstrated performance, expected replication and deployed capacity. Certification/insurance, field-control metrology and Mc scarcity are supported constraints; a new rare element or surface-only cut-off is **not** needed. I §5.3 and II §18A distinguish mature Metric from tactical vector control.

**D. Gameplay.** Choose lift vs conserve field margin; low-speed stealthy extraction vs long exposed ascent; when to risk hover; where to demonstrate; whether to abort on unstable boundary readings. Clocks: electrical power, hold/thermal and certification margin, tracking exposure. Failure: loss of lift, boundary asymmetry, water/air entrainment, uncontrolled descent. Counterplay: track the plume-free craft kinematically (II §18A), pressure ports/insurance, jam or deny trusted metrology without claiming a remote physics kill. A 29 MWe reactor is **not** proof of an available 29 MWe drive input after other ship loads.

**E. Story.** Wayfarer's packet/information value justifies an unusual prototype. Public kinematics but private field telemetry invite bidding, espionage, fraud and bubbles; see economy lane §§1–6. Nursery/ʻOumuamua fears and rumours of Builder replication are powerful **character beliefs**, not evidence of causal Builder ancestry. Preserve hidden truth separately. Insurers and rival programs may be more dangerous than pirates.

**F. Smallest decisive test.** One **closed** local-partner cycle with start/end positions, velocities, field state, body impulse, work, heat and stored energy equalized. Report net work and any missing constitutive term. Do not build a generalized engine.

## A–F. Option 2 — Short Loom jumps

**A. Miracles and authority.** Relational reattachment rather than ordinary travel is already I §§6.2, 6.6–6.8 and Weave §§10–13, 15–16. The proposal that nearby places are relationally indistinguishable, and fine-selectivity lattices can overcome it, is a **lane hypothesis**; neither the claimed 'far easy, near hard' rule nor kilometre-scale attachment is certified canon. II §12.1 locks committed membership and configuration. I §6.7 formation is minutes or longer and visible; transition can be sub-second. I §6.11 forbids emergence into incompatible macroscopically occupied volumes; air and water remain unresolved. I §6.12 permits reversion, displacement, decoherence and non-return. II §6 near-body qualification is not automatically solved. I §5.3 is Metric, not Loom permission.

**B. Conservation.** I §6.8 expressly disallows a net-energy-producing translation cycle. Upward hops must account for gravitational potential and arrival state; Loom is not free braking. II §7.3 demands the field, environment and source ledgers. A hop up, harvest fall, and return to the original state is the exact adversarial test; if the jump is 'cheap' but harvest is positive, the hypothesis fails. The ordinary metric Sun-frame ENERGY-B equation in II §7.1a is **not** automatically the Loom arrival law. Do not silently transplant it.

**C. Scope.** Successful fine-grained jumps could eclipse local transport, terrestrial logistics, boarding and security, potentially even sealift. But the option brief's existing scarcity is severe: I §5.4 describes only dozens of Loom drives; II §12.1 requires fixed domain state; a shuttle-sized lattice is speculative. The least disruptive immediate application is a Wayfarer-sized lattice making body-to-body vacuum jumps, leaving shuttle descent conventional. No assertion of universal short-hop availability.

**D. Gameplay.** Choose departure geometry, target density, formation site and whether to trust a low-confidence endpoint; weigh minutes of exposed formation against a sub-second transition. Failure: pre-commit abort, reversion, wrong admissible endpoint, fatal decoherence (I §§6.7, 6.11–6.12). Counterplay: track the conspicuous formation, contest admissible landing volumes, monitor re-emergence. Observers see formation and disappearance, not a streak between endpoints.

**E. Story.** The most valuable asset may be a fine-resolution relational map, not the drive. A leaked endpoint solution could collapse navigation monopolies; Builder evidence may collapse research search-space, but its origin and significance remain uncertain. False claims of 'jump into a sealed vault' conflict with I §6.11 and create useful scams. Nursery-linked rumours remain beliefs, not canon facts.

**F. Smallest decisive test.** On a **fixed existing domain** (no invented shuttle lattice), evaluate two candidate endpoints within one star system against the already-authored relational mismatch/admissibility concept (Weave §§11–13). Report whether they are distinguishable and what is **not specified** by the current model. If the substrate lacks intra-system resolution, stop; do not invent a lattice algorithm.

## A–F. Option 3 — Weave manoeuvring ('tic-tac')

**A. Miracles and authority.** Best direct fit to M1/M2 as a **hypothetical operating mode**, not a third miracle: Weave §7 fixes relational attachment while continuously deforming the metric; §8 has no remass; §17 WCS is inverse control, not new physics. I §5.3 explicitly permits coordinate displacement without equivalent crew acceleration. But no source certifies sub-second ramps, near-body agile motion or a free dead stop. II §9 defines long clean-environment ramp paths (NORMAL two-ramp minimum ~0.803 million km); §6 requires environmental certification; §18A says Metric is **not** a close-combat dodge; §14 prohibits simultaneous major torch/high-Metric operation. II §7.1a is a scoped Sun-barycentric **arrival-velocity** rule, not a universal near-body motion law. II §12.1 applies if Loom commitment is added, not to pure Weave manoeuvring.

**B. Conservation.** The option brief identifies a serious existing pump: under the current ENERGY-B interpretation a 1 km metric climb near Earth can trade ~0.33 m/s of Sun-frame speed, while harvesting an ordinary fall yields ~0.6 GJ/km for 61 t (commit b44a114). That is **not** closed physics: it is a contradiction candidate against II §7.3 and I §6.8's no-free-work principle (the latter specifically governs Loom sequences). II §7.1b itself warns that Sun momentum/energy balancing identities are not an independent derivation and retains a MONITOR cycle residual. A near-body Weave mode must explicitly pay gravitational potential and any harvestable cycle work, or fail. Calling it a geometric path does not evade the ledger.

**C. Scope.** If fast, crew-safe and reliable, this is not merely a shuttle feature: tactical vector changes, terminal interception, low-altitude flight, orbital insertion and security doctrine all change. II §18A is the clearest setting-level collision. Rarity can limit deployment but cannot erase what observers infer. A genuine ramp floor or thermal/control limit is an engineering limitation **to discover**, not a convenient invented cap.

**D. Gameplay.** Pilot selects a manoeuvre corridor, field amplitude, ramp timing, thermal margin and abort threshold. Dramatic outcomes: plume-free sudden reposition, hovering without crew loading, dead stop; visible discontinuity in tracked kinematics and possible thermal/EM signatures (II §18A). Counterplay: prediction of ramp/thermal clocks, constrained geometry, metrology denial, weapons timed for field transition. Failure: asymmetric field collapse, environmental interaction, uncontrolled momentum/position, thermal exhaustion. Do **not** assert any numerical subsecond envelope before test.

**E. Story.** A prototype 'tic-tac' filmed near a port becomes instant political and financial evidence; militaries want it, insurers refuse it, Builder/Nursery conspiracies attach to it. Rivalry over WCS calibration and field-control data may matter more than hull ownership. Public demonstrations cannot conceal kinematics (II §18A).

**F. Smallest decisive test.** Take one 1 km up/down metric manoeuvre and include complete Sun, Earth, shuttle, field-bank, heat and harvested-energy states. Require no net extractable work under II §7.3. Only if it closes, test a **single** short ramp against II §§6, 9 and 18A. Do not run an entire tactical flight suite.

## Combination check: 2 + 3 via Weave seams

Weave §19 defines **hypothetical** seams where multiple external attachments have nearly degenerate admissibility, and §10 distinguishes continuous Metric from relational Loom transitions. A hybrid is narratively compelling: agile Weave approach to a seam, then fine-selectivity Loom reattachment. But **it is not presently cleaner physics**: it inherits option 3's energy-pump problem, option 2's endpoint resolution/admissibility problem, and II §12.1's fixed committed domain. Weave §14 also requires Metric collapse → relax/rephase → Loom formation; no instantaneous mid-field hybrid is authorized. The combination is worth a **later** test only if both separate decisive unknowns are resolved.

- **Closest existing mechanism:** 3 (Weave), but its currently exposed energy pump is a hard blocker.
- **Most conservative hard invariant:** 2 has explicit I §6.8 no-free-work doctrine, but no evidence of intra-system endpoint discrimination.
- **Most directly useful shuttle prototype and strongest economic intrigue:** 1, but its local partner and cost law are new unproven constitutive permissions.
- **No winner.** kT holds all three. The first work is three **small falsification tests**, not a new physical framework, shuttle design, canon amendment or mass ledger.
- **Stop here:** ranking and decisive unknowns. No mutations to PR #19 or #16.
