## 1. Can a handful of prototypes trigger serious speculation? Yes, and canon already predicts how
**Canon mechanisms that already exist:**
- **Sol markets are efficient, and opportunity appears at shocks and information asymmetry** (CANON I §11.8). Markets price expectations quickly.
- **The Artifact Economy is the precedent** (§11.10). New finds create valuation shocks in "materials, biotech, aerospace, insurance, provenance, compute" even when "not every find produces a miracle technology".
  - **Attention Intelligence** infers what rivals think matters from hiring, isotope orders, instrument purchases and compute allocation.
  - **Negative results are strategically priceless.**
- **Institutional response to Loom** (§3.4): export controls, classification, patents, licensing, insurance restrictions, military planning.

**A demonstration cannot be hidden:**
- **Kinematics leak** (CANON II §18A sensor primacy): stealth "does not erase thermal, kinematic or electromagnetic physics".
- A 61 t craft hovering for an hour, or climbing to orbit with no plume and no mass loss, is visible to every tracking network in Sol.
- **What stays private is the performance data:**
  - the capacity law;
  - power draw;
  - failure modes;
  - field-control know-how.
- That gap between public kinematics and private performance is exactly the asymmetry §11.8 says creates opportunity.

**Where the demonstration happens changes the market:**
- **In Sol:** repricing within light-time. Rumour becomes public evidence in hours.
- **Across a Loom edge:** news reaches Sol only by packet (CANON II §18). Whoever carries the first authenticated packet holds tradable information, and the market runs on causally old rumour until it arrives.

That is the most LOOM-native version of the bubble.

## 2. Industries and institutions most affected
**Geography first.** Under the tested law, capacity is always 1.5 × local weight at a surface, but the useful absolute acceleration follows gravity.

| Region | Field capacity | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Earth / Venus surface to orbit | 1.5 / 1.36 g | **Highest.** Launch, landing and orbital insertion; the gravity-well tax disappears. |
| Jupiter / Saturn moon systems | ~4.6 km/s per day at Callisto; ~3.3 km/s per day at Titan's orbit | **High.** Intermoon logistics (Callisto yards, Saturn treaty institutions). |
| Inner heliocentric space | 5.1 km/s per day at Mercury's orbit; 1.5 at Venus; 0.77 at Earth | **High near the Sun** (Mercury process/metrology economy). |
| Mars surface / Mars orbit | 0.57 g; 0.33 km/s per day | Medium. |
| Belt (Ceres) | 0.04 g surface; 0.10 km/s per day | **Low technically.** Bulk freight stays torch. |
| Outer heliocentric transfers | 0.01–0.03 km/s per day | Negligible. |

**Exposure by actor.** All of these are canon actors.

**Repricing earliest:**
- **Reaction-mass producers and tankers:** Ceres water/remass, the Callisto remass yards, the reaction-mass tanker fleet.
  - They stay profitable for years.
  - Their *long-term* valuations fall first, because inner-system and gravity-well demand is what the expectation removes.
- **Strategic sealift** (CANON I §11.7.1; Atlas §16.1): the commercial torch fleet *is* the reserve.
  - Governments will treat gravity steering as a sealift and military question before it is a commercial one.
  - Expect classification and export controls (§3.4).

**The six corporate lineages (Atlas §16):**
- **Asteria Ship Systems** (integration): torch-system makers face valuation pressure; it is also a natural rival integrator.
- **Axiom Precision & Metrology:** field control and certification are the binding know-how, so Axiom is a structural winner and a prime espionage target.
- **Helios Materials:** gains if the domain needs ultra-pure relational materials.
- **Ferrum Meridian:** isotope/industrial scale; Mc supply exposure.
- **Concord Mutual Infrastructure & Assurance:** decides whether the technology is commercially usable at all (§11.3). It can render prototypes "commercially radioactive". Gray insurance covers uncertified drives.

**Other institutions:**
- **Insurers and underwriters:** uncertainty premium on torch hulls and remass contracts; no cover for the prototypes; fraud screening.
- **Mc-299m holders and allocators** (CANON II §3.1): see §3.
- **Earth–Mars rivalry** (§3.4): competing state programmes are the default, not an invention.

## 3. Which scarcity assumptions are actually necessary
The physics stays general (no new restriction). Ranked by canon support:

1. **Certification and insurance gate.** *Fully canon.*
   - "A new uncertified part can be physically excellent yet institutionally unusable" (CANON I, physical condition vs confidence).
   - Insurer refusal outweighs government permission in practice (§11.3).
   - CANON III §5: "Worldbuilding must be regenerated from the certified propulsion stack." **Uncertified prototypes do not regenerate the world economy; expectations do.**
   - This single canon rule produces the gap kT wants between demonstration and deployment.
2. **Field-control and metrology know-how.** *Canon-consistent.*
   - Metric hardware is "precision control systems" (§5.3).
   - The industrial stack peaks at metrology and certification (Atlas §16).
   - Knowledge held by a few teams is scarce, stealable and the real target of espionage.
3. **Mc-299m relational hardware.** *Canon scarcity, if gravity steering is an M2 operating mode.*
   - Serviceable flow is about 1.2 t/yr, and demand already exceeds supply (CANON II §3.1).
   - At the L² candidate scaling a shuttle domain needs about 1.5 kg. That is **no barrier to a handful**, but a real brake on scale: it competes with metric and Loom commissioning queues.
   - *The 1.5 kg is an unverified candidate fit.*
4. **Builder-derived insight.** *Optional.* Canon says artifacts "collapse search space rather than handing over a working technology" (§4.5).
   - A prototype lineage traced to Builder evidence would add custody, provenance and partition politics.
   - It is not needed for scarcity.

**Not needed:** rare new materials, arbitrary physical caps, or any rule that protects remass. Items 1–3 are enough.

## 4. Smallest useful scenario (all counts are scenario assumptions, not canon)
**Actors** (all existing canon institutions):
- **Wayfarer's shuttle:** the most-instrumented prototype, justified by mission information value.
- **One state programme:** Earth or Mars, inside the Earth–Mars rivalry.
- **One corporate programme:** for example Asteria integration with Axiom metrology.

**Demonstration:**
- An **exhaust-free lift and one-hour hover** of the 61 t shuttle at sea.
- Optionally, a slow climb to orbit.
- It is publicly observable; the performance data is private.

**What each layer looks like:**

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Demonstrated** | One 61 t craft; lift, hover, maybe orbit; one capacity law observed indirectly. |
| **Expected** | Market and institutional belief about time-to-certification and cost per unit (Mc, metrology, insurance). |
| **Deployed** | **Zero commercial tonnes.** |

**Reactions to run, using existing systems:**

**Instability triggers:**
- A second successful trial.
- A failure. The W3/BMD notes record asymmetric field collapse as catastrophic.
- A leak or a fraudulent claim.
- Mc allocation news.

## 5. Concrete limitations before any new machinery
- **Existing systems model flows, contracts, reputations and evidence, not beliefs.**
  - The one missing piece is a **technology-credibility state**: what each institution believes about the technology and its time-to-scale.
  - The smallest form reuses canon's **evidence-state discipline** (Atlas §4.2 / CANON I §4.2: separate states, provenance, independent replication) as a short ladder: rumour → specialist evidence → public demonstration → independent replication → certified.
  - It needs one expectation value per affected sector.
  - This is a proposal, not built.
- **No valuation or asset-price model exists, and none is proposed.** Use qualitative repricing events.

## 6. Canon changes that would improve coherence, choices and narrative (kT decides; none made)
1. **Decide the technology's ontological status.**
   - Either an M2 operating mode (a CCR scoping §7.1a/§7.1b exceptions for near-body powered domains),
   - or a separate Builder-derived prototype class.
   - The first keeps the two-miracle budget.
2. **Tie it to Mc-299m and NRE relational hardware.** This uses canon scarcity rather than inventing one, and makes it compete with metric and Loom.
3. **Add an "uncertified experimental drive" status** to the certification and insurance doctrine. Canon already has gray insurance; it lacks a named prototype class.
4. **Amend §18A** ("metric is not a close-combat dodge") and the §5 near-body certification wording for the prototype class only.
5. **State the world-propagation consequence explicitly:** uncertified prototypes change expectations, not the certified propulsion stack (CANON III §5). That keeps 2226 intact while letting markets move.
