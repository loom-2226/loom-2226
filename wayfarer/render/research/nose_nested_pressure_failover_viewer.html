Static failover logic

During normal operation the inter-pane gap is evacuated and isolated. If the inner primary fails, cavity air pressure rises and the intact outer pressure dome becomes the backup. This graphic shows stable end states only. Actual rupture, shock loading and glass debris are not simulated.

Real engineering unknowns

Each pressure shell's own rim must accept the entire ~3.90 MN axial pressure resultant upon failure of the other. The unloaded backup in normal operations is **not** an unused decorative pane; its flange and seal are critical.

This model assumes full-pressure-capable shells based on a previous *ideal membrane* analysis, not actual failure-tested glass. The transient after primary fracture could impact or fracture the backup; the seal response, shock and crack fragmentation are not modeled.

The interspace must stay isolated from an open-to-vacuum vent. Otherwise inner-pane failure could leak cabin air through that vent rather than pressurising the backup cavity. Periodic evacuation with a closed isolation path is one possible operating concept, but no valve is designed here.

Outer protective tiles and inner pressure shells use independent attachment systems and the baseline material density allocations. No change to source PR432, crew stations, shielding, or canon.
