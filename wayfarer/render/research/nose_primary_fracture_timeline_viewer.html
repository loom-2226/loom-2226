WAYFARER · PRIMARY-PANE FRACTURE CHALLENGE

Held 37 replaceable exterior tiles · two seamless pressure shells · one assumed 0.25m² breach and detached fragment · non-canon research

Fragment travel versus backup pressure

Fragment follows the schematic normal direction. The backup is 5.97 cm away at the smallest clearance location; the graphic expands this gap for readability. Fragment stops in the drawing at contact, but its real collision and any subsequent motion are unmodeled.

Uniform-cavity pressure history

Model is choked-to-subcritical ideal gas flow through an assumed 0.25m² constant opening into a 3.02m³ cavity, with constant cabin pressure and temperature. The gap begins evacuated: sound cannot propagate through the vacuum. The 0.174ms distance/sound-speed comparison is NOT an arrival-time calculation; the real fracture creates a compressible jet and expansion front.

What this does NOT show

There is no verified impact failure threshold for the backup dome. One 21.3kg piece is an invented diagnostic fragment, not a predicted fragmentation pattern. The model excludes released shell strain energy, pane flexure, shard breakup and gas-jet forces.

Both pressure domes are independently sealed in the static concept. The external 37 protective tiles are unchanged and do not bear cabin pressure.

The decisive issue is whether primary-pane fragments or pressure shocks initiate backup-pane failure. A static pressure design cannot answer that, and neither can this deliberately simple one-piece motion test.
