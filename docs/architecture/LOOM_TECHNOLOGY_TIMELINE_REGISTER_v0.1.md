## 1. How to interpret a threshold (mandatory)

Kevin selected the **moderate** column of a conservative/moderate/aggressive scenario assessment as the working *scenario scaffold*, not a forecast or a fixed world-history script. Every numeric year in §4 is an **author-proposed scenario anchor**, not a date predicted by the linked research. External sources establish demonstrated precursor capabilities, public program objectives or engineering obstacles; the exact future year is an extrapolation. In particular, a program's aspirational target, license, planned launch or research milestone is NOT evidence that it achieved the target.

`threshold_year` means the moderate scenario's indicative **first sustained practical achievement of the exact named capability**, subject to prerequisites, investment, engineering success, material flows, local plant and relevant certification. Some rows explicitly require a paying or otherwise economically sustainable operation; an experiment cannot satisfy them. The date alone does **not** unlock a technology, create capacity, give every agent access, establish profitability for another process, cause migration or colonize a location. Agents can research, invest and attempt prototypes before the year; the model can discover earlier/later results from its own mechanisms. If a model deliberately uses dates as scenario priors, record that assumption openly and permit failed or delayed achievement. If nothing is built, a threshold does nothing. **Never silently convert this document into compulsory event scheduling.**

Keep four separate states: `knowledge_or_demonstration`, `reliable_operational_design`, `installed_local_capacity`, `actor_access_and_adoption`. A working component need not be mass manufacturable, certifiable, affordable or universally available. For each agent, site and year check physical feasibility, qualified design, materials/fuel, energy and heat, manufacture/construction/repair, transport and logistics, finance, legal rights, assurance and reliability. Costs require currency, base year, denominator and scope; source research here does not provide usable numeric technology cost curves. Missing quantities remain `UNKNOWN` / null, not zero. The 2026 starting state must be calibrated from empirical world data independently; future fiction cannot fill baseline gaps.

**Hard boundary:** Existing canon is a **comparator**, not a destination. Do not tune technology, demographics, synthetic counts, GDP, geography, institutions, fleet size, discovery or 2226 outcomes backward to match the fictional setting. Earth is the empirical origin. Earth orbit/Luna/Mars are investment opportunities; none is an assured success or stepping-stone. All subsequent destinations and development are endogenous. A failed technology or no off-world settlement is an admissible model result. No alien event, metric drive or Loom miracle is scientifically predicted.

## 3. Precursor evidence and limits

Real precursors already exist: the FAA's Starship review addresses **up to 25 annual launches/landings**, not 25 achieved launches or mature economic reusability [R01]; NASA's MW-class nuclear-electric architecture identified immature critical subsystems and possible late-2030s/2040s mission windows, not a qualified fleet [R02]. NASA describes a **40-kW-class lunar fission system** targeted for the early 2030s, not a demonstrated MW industrial installation [R03]. ITER's approved research baseline schedules DT operations for **2039**, not net electric power [R04]. IEA's 2025 STEPS is an exploratory *Earth electricity* scenario with renewables over half of generation by 2035, not a guarantee of cheap power in space [R05]. NASA's lunar drilling/resource characterization [R06], Mars MOXIE oxygen demonstration [R07], robotic regolith construction work [R08], and a proposed uncrewed lunar industrial park [R09] do not establish profitable mines or self-building industries. IFR records 542,000 industrial robot installations in 2024 [R10]; an AI-author survey sharply separates broad task ability from automating occupations and contains substantial uncertainty [R11]. ISS water recovery reached 98% in a subsystem in 2023 [R12], not complete food/air/spares closure. NASA space-crop work still identifies production hurdles [R13]. NASA's LunaNet standards/relays and operational DTN [R14–R15] do not remove speed-of-light latency. Human partial-gravity health and long-term reproduction remain insufficiently demonstrated; treat proposed medical dates as particularly conjectural [R16–R17].

### 4.1 Synthetic-person legal recognition: separate social scenario, NOT a technology threshold

**2090 — optional moderate *first-jurisdiction recognition* anchor**, as floated in the prior scenario assessment. It is an expressly **author-created social-history hypothesis, not an empirically supported technological forecast** and not a mandatory event in the simulation. It neither creates synthetic persons nor establishes consciousness, sentience, industrial capability, universal rights, demographic growth or a globally uniform legal regime. An institutional agent must actually enact recognition; cognition/instantiation, substrate, compute, embodiment, legal personhood and non-person task-equivalent capacity remain separate states. If no such actor acts, the date passes without change. [S0,S1 §10.2,S4,R11].

### 4.2 Institutional access and research: deliberately no universal calendar year

`ACC` depends on jurisdiction, finance, liability, IP, insurance, qualification and ownership, per actor and installation. `SCI` depends on research investment, experimental capacity and reproducible evidence. There is no single global date for either. Agents may create institutions, finance experiments or impose restrictions before or after every scenario anchor. Missing adoption lags and cost curves remain OPEN, not assumed zero.

## 6. Minimal dependency and agent-decision contract

`research + capital -> tested design -> energy + qualified materials + manufacturing + construction + repair -> installed local capacity -> viable transport/extraction/automation/habitation -> agent choices`, with investment, revenue, institutional access, competition, learning and discoveries feeding back. This is a **candidate modeling structure**, not a compulsory one-way historical sequence. Do not infer local manufacturing from local ore, industrial autonomy from robotic cognition, human settlement from habitable beds, or profitable asteroid extraction from probe access. A synthetic person may be an autonomous economic/political actor; a non-person robot-equivalent is a task-capacity stock, never a person added to census. Biological persistence additionally requires measured health and reproductive feasibility in a specified environment.

For each candidate site and mode maintain physical reach, available service/annual throughput, recurrent logistics and emergency return as separate propositions. No colony is mandated. In the model, each technology candidate competes for funding; a threshold can be delayed or skipped by actual prerequisites. Report scenario-year achievement, nonachievement and deviation rather than forcing chronology into place.
