# AUS-ACCESS-1: Australian Government lunar access

**Qualified:** the `AUS` country actor, through the Australian Space Agency,
participates in the government Roo-ver lunar demonstration. NASA's
[2021 agency agreement](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-australia-sign-agreement-to-add-rover-to-future-moon-mission/)
is rover-specific and conditional. The [Australian Government's August 2025
mission announcement](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/t-ayres/media/mission-confirmed-aussie-moon-rover-ready-roll)
commits AUD 42 million to Roo-ver development and operation in partnership with
the Agency. NASA's [27 March 2026 CLPS award](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/clps/nasa-selects-intuitive-machines-to-deliver-artemis-science-tech-to-moon/)
includes Roo-ver, names the Agency as its lead organization, and selects Intuitive
Machines for end-to-end South Pole region delivery targeted for 2030. This is
**observed access to one partner-provided government mission pathway**, not
Australian ownership of a lander, NASA's provider contract, or arbitrary lunar
payload capacity. [Agency reporting in August 2026](https://www.space.gov.au/news-and-media/critical-testing-begins-for-roo-ver-before-moon-journey-in-2030)
describes the project as still in testing and partnered with NASA.

The existing resolver needed only a second evidence packet and selection by
actor and capability. Generic `AUS` access is **USABLE within the named Roo-ver
scope**. CIVPROP-0's separate synthetic prospecting payload, 28 September 2026
departure and unspecified polar site are **CONDITIONAL** for access: no matching
slot, payload accommodation, site coverage, operations arrangement or price is
established, and the documented Roo-ver mission targets 2030. Conditional access
does not clear transport, so CIVPROP-0 returns transport **UNKNOWN** and decision
**WAIT** without expenditure; replay remains deterministic.

The Fleet/SPIDER agreement remains Fleet's, not the government's. The Moon to
Mars grant alone would not qualify; Roo-ver also has a NASA/Agency mission
relationship and a CLPS delivery award. Artemis Accords membership and the
broader [Australia–US Space Framework Agreement](https://www.space.gov.au/international-partnerships)
do not grant a separate payload slot. A NASA [2025 agreement register](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/house-appropriations-international-3-31-2025.pdf)
lists a Roo-ver design-review agreement expiring 30 April 2026; current renewal
paperwork is unverified. The later CLPS award and 2026 Agency project reporting
support a continuing mission pathway, but no general contractual entitlement is
inferred from them.
