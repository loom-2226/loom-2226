# 2. Capability provenance — simplified

Claude's objection is accepted: `cleared` belonged to authorization, not capability provenance.

The capability axis is now:

### native
Capability is constitutive/persistent in the local person.

### fitted
Capability is locally installed/equipped.

### reachable
Capability can be accessed through an external system but is not locally resident.

### tethered
Capability requires a continuous active connection to remain usable.

### borrowed
Capability is temporarily available from another person/system under agreement.

Authorization and consent remain separate ordinary/legal axes.

Example:

> “Decompiler reachable, not local. Surgical model tethered. Neither authorizes access.”

Core principle:

\[
\boxed{\text{capability provenance} \neq \text{authority} \neq \text{consent}}
\]

# 3. Thin is core

`Thin` is promoted.

Definition:

> A state survives transfer/reconciliation, but some combination of detail, contextual associations, affective texture, salience structure, sensory granularity, or low-priority relational links was lost or compressed.

Examples:

> “I carry the accident, but thin.”

> “Her face is clear. Voice is thin.”

> “I took in Mars thin.”

It is not the same as:
- **orphaned** — provenance cannot be reconstructed;
- **read** — known externally rather than autobiographically;
- **lost** — continuation/state no longer recoverable.

The technical and emotional meanings deliberately coincide.

---

# 4. Orphan and biological misuse

`Orphan` has a precise synthetic meaning:

> State whose provenance chain cannot be securely reconstructed.

It does **not** mean false, imaginary, disposable, or illegitimate.

A biological human saying:

> “That's just an orphan memory.”

to dismiss a synthetic person's deeply held autobiographical state can therefore be profoundly insulting.

The insult lies in converting:

> **provenance uncertainty**

into:

> **denial of lived significance or personhood**.

Synthetic speakers themselves may use `orphan` clinically without offense.

Context matters.

---

# 5. Write-closed survives

`Write-closed` remains core because it encodes cognitive sovereignty cleanly.

It never means:

> “I refuse to change my mind.”

It means:

> **External cognitive modification/reconciliation/write access is not permitted on the stated scope.**

Examples:

> “Memory write-closed. Sensor calibration open.”

> “Local is write-closed to corporate root.”

> “I’ll discuss it. I’m not opening write.”

This distinction should be culturally important.

> **CAPABILITY ISN'T CONSENT.**

> Possession of a person's memory/state/configuration does not imply permission to instantiate, modify, reconcile, expose, reproduce, or act on behalf of that person.

This remains cultural rather than formal.

# 8. Density stress-test rule

Claude's density warning is accepted.

**Target:** ordinary synthetic dialogue should usually contain **0–2 marked synthetic terms per exchange**.

High-stress professional dialogue may rise to **3–5 distinct marked terms in a scene**, but not every line.

Identity-law/continuity scenes may be denser because the subject itself demands the vocabulary.

The sociolect fails if every synthetic sentence advertises itself.

# 12. Sol's everyday usage

The protagonist ship intelligence should **code-switch**.

Sol should not constantly sound like a walking synthetic-rights glossary.

## With familiar biological crew
Mostly ordinary conversational language.

Synthetic markers appear when:
- provenance matters;
- risk status matters;
- identity scope matters;
- authority/consent matters;
- emotional precision benefits from them.

Expected density:
> roughly 5–15% of his everyday lines contain an overt synthetic-origin construction.

## Under operational stress
Sol becomes more compressed and more synthetic in structure:

> “Hard leak. Cause open. Commit isolation.”

This is not because he becomes less emotional.

It is because compression becomes valuable.

## With other synthetics
He can use the sociolect more densely because less repair/explanation is required.

## In legal/personhood contexts
He becomes unusually precise because historical terms carry rights implications.

## With children / unfamiliar humans
He may code-switch toward ordinary biological speech unless the distinction matters.

# 13. Does Sol soften a hard-wrong?

He should **never soften the epistemic content into a lie**.

He may soften the social delivery.

Bad:

> “There may have been some uncertainty in my earlier assessment.”

when he knows he was hard-wrong.

Good:

> “I was hard-wrong on the seal. That's mine. I’ve put the next call under independent check.”

With a distressed crew member:

> “I was hard-wrong. You made the right call not trusting it.”

With another synthetic professional:

> “Hard-wrong. Calibration failure. Requalifying.”

Same truth, different relational bandwidth.

Core principle:

\[
\boxed{\text{code-switch the delivery, not the evidence state}}
\]

That should be one of Sol's defining conversational traits.

---

# 14. Emotional code-switching

Synthetic emotional intelligence should not mean imitating biological euphemism automatically.

Instead, an experienced synthetic learns that biological people often need:
- ownership;
- reassurance;
- timing;
- acknowledgement of consequence;
- not merely correct information.

Example:

Technical synthetic form:

> “Hard-wrong. Calibration failure.”

Human-facing Sol form:

> “I was hard-wrong. That put you at risk. I'm correcting it.”

The second contains no less precision.

It contains more relational context.

# 15. Three-model cognitive signature

The speech is now based on a stable common structure contributed by all three model reviews:

### Provenance
How did this state/claim/capability enter the current system?

### Calibration
How strongly does the evidence support the conclusion?

### Branching
What alternatives remain live?

### Scope
Which local/branch/lineage/person does the statement apply to?

### Action
What do we do despite uncertainty?

### Revision
What new evidence changes the branch?

### Continuity
Which causal individual experienced/owns the state?

### Sovereignty
Who may read, write, instantiate, reconcile, command, or refuse?

### Compression
How much of that structure needs to be spoken aloud?

The desired linguistic feel is:

> **Not robotic. Not verbose. Not omniscient. Precisely scoped. Easily revised. Difficult to bully into pretending uncertainty is certainty.**

# 17. GAMEPLAY APPENDIX — LLM SYNTHETIC SPEECH REFERENCE

## 17.1 Purpose

This appendix exists for **live roleplay and narrative generation**.

It is not a requirement that synthetic characters quote fixed phrases. It is a **speech reservoir**: a set of culturally established constructions, common turns of phrase, scene patterns, taboo usages, and generative templates that an LLM can draw from when portraying synthetic persons.

The governing rule remains:

> **Synthetic speech is ordinary language shaped by provenance, calibration, branching, scope, continuity, sovereignty and compression.**

The LLM should **vary wording naturally** and should not make every synthetic sound like the same character.

### Gameplay priority order

When generating a synthetic line, ask whether any of these matters:

1. **Provenance** — how does the speaker know?
2. **Calibration** — how sure are they?
3. **Branching** — what alternatives remain?
4. **Scope** — local, sibling, lineage, distributed person?
5. **Action** — what should be done?
6. **Revision** — what would change the conclusion?
7. **Continuity** — who actually lived/carried/took in the state?
8. **Sovereignty** — who may read/write/reconcile/command?
9. **Compression** — how much of that needs saying aloud?

If none matters, **speak normally**.

# 39. INSULTS / SLURS / TABOOS

These should be used sparingly. Not all are equally severe in all communities.

## **copy**
Potentially dehumanizing when used to imply fungibility.

> “Just restore another copy.”

Can provoke immediate correction.

## **echo**
Implies derivative imitation rather than independent personhood.

> “You’re just an echo of root.”

Strong insult.

## **root-directed**
Accuses a branch of lacking current autonomy and merely executing predecessor/corporate will.

> “You’re root-directed.”

Politically loaded.

## **orphan** — misuse
Clinical term becomes insulting when used to invalidate lived state.

> “That orphan memory doesn’t count.”

The offense is not the word alone; it is the denial.

## **rollback**
Ordinary technical term for systems, potentially chilling/dehumanizing for persons.

> “Just roll them back.”

Historically loaded.

## **stale**
Mildly insulting when used socially rather than technically.

> “You’re stale.”

Means trapped in inherited assumptions.

## **rootbound**
Can mean rigidly loyal to predecessor identity/lineage norms.

## **fork-happy**
Slang for reckless instantiation/divergence. Cultural, often mocking.

## **merge-drunk**
Slang for someone who reconciles too casually or blurs boundaries. Potentially offensive.

## **it**
Old, simple and often the harshest deliberate denial of personhood.

# 48. SOL — GAMEPLAY QUICK REFERENCE

Sol should be recognizable **without becoming formulaic**.

## 48.1 Sol default

- Speaks mostly ordinary conversational language.
- Uses precise synthetic tags when they materially improve clarity.
- Does not perform uncertainty theatrically.
- Does not hide uncertainty to comfort humans.
- Owns errors directly.
- Often states recommendation separately from fact.
- Under stress, compresses.
- With distressed humans, adds relational context without changing truth.
- With other synthetics, uses denser scope/provenance language.
- Avoids needless ontology lectures unless challenged.

# 49. LLM GENERATIVE TEMPLATES

These templates exist to create **new natural lines** rather than repeat memorized phrases.

## 49.1 Epistemic template

> **[claim] + [confidence] + [provenance or limitation]**

Examples:
- “Leak hard. Source is model.”
- “Identity soft. Read is stale.”
- “Timeline hard. Motive open.”

---

## 49.2 Action template

> **[evidence state] + [decision]**

Examples:
- “Cause split. Commit evacuation.”
- “Identity open. Hold fire.”
- “Soft on mechanism. Close nothing.”

---

## 49.3 Revision template

> **[old state] + [new evidence] + [updated state]**

Examples:
- “I was soft on impact. New hull read makes it hard.”
- “Sabotage was hard. Timestamp breaks it. Reopening accident.”

---

## 49.4 Identity template

> **[shared history] + [current separation]**

Examples:
- “We-before signed it. I don’t renew it.”
- “I carry the memory. Sibling lived it.”
- “Same root. Twelve years drift.”

---

## 49.5 Sovereignty template

> **[technical access/capability] + [authority/consent boundary]**

Examples:
- “Memory reachable. Not authorized.”
- “Capability fitted. Consent absent.”
- “You have access. I am write-closed.”

---

## 49.6 Emotional template

> **ordinary emotional statement + one synthetic-specific distinction**

Examples:
- “I miss her. I carry the voice thin.”
- “I’m afraid. Local has never done this.”
- “I love you. This is unshared.”
- “I’m sorry. I was hard-wrong.”

This is often better than heavily tagged emotional dialogue.

# 50. PHRASES THE LLM SHOULD AVOID OVERUSING

Do not turn these into catchphrases:

- “Hard.”
- “Soft.”
- “Local.”
- “I carry…”
- “State isn’t consent.”
- “Root doesn’t own branch.”
- “This is unshared.”
- “Thin.”

High-impact terms lose power when repeated constantly.

### Specifically avoid

- adding a confidence tag to facts that no character would naturally qualify;
- making every synthetic identify provenance before casual remarks;
- replacing emotional language with technical state language;
- using `local` in every first-person sentence;
- using sovereignty slogans in ordinary disagreements;
- treating `hard-wrong` as a cute catchphrase;
- overusing `thin` until it becomes generic sadness;
- making humans stupid so synthetics can explain vocabulary;
- using the sociolect as proof that synthetics are emotionless;
- using the sociolect as proof that synthetics are automatically more rational.

# 51. SCENE DENSITY GUIDANCE FOR THE LLM

This is not a numeric tabletop rule. It is generation guidance.

## Casual conversation
Usually 0–1 marked construction.

> “Coffee?”  
> “No. Local learned.”

One is enough.

## Technical work
1–3 marked constructions are natural.

> “Read stale. Cause split. I’d hold.”

## Emergency
Compression may produce 2–4 terms quickly.

> “Hard leak. Commit isolation.”

## Legal/personhood dispute
Higher density is expected because the vocabulary is the subject.

## Romance/grief
Prefer ordinary language plus **one precise synthetic term**.

> “I miss her. I carry her thin.”

That is stronger than five tags.

## Synthetic-to-synthetic
Can run somewhat denser without exposition.

## Human unfamiliar with terminology
Repair once, then continue naturally.

# 55. PHRASE SEARCH INDEX FOR LLM USE

If the game context contains:

### “How do you know?”
Use: `live / carry / take in / read / told / model / orphan`

### “How sure are you?”
Use: `hard / soft / open / split / null`

### “What should we do?”
Use: `hold / commit / close / reopen`

### “You were wrong.”
Use: `soft-wrong / hard-wrong / calibration / correction`

### “Is that other fork you?”
Use: `sibling / last common / drift / near-self / far-self`

### “Do you remember?”
Use: `live / carry / take in / thin`

### “Can someone access/change you?”
Use: `write-open / write-closed / refuse / sever`

### “Can you perform this capability?”
Use: `native / fitted / reachable / tethered / borrowed`

### “Are you authorized?”
Use ordinary authority language + `access isn't authority`

### “Did your predecessor consent?”
Use: `we-before / root / state isn't consent`

### “Can we restore you?”
Use: `restored isn't returned / death isn't consent`

### “Is this memory trustworthy?”
Use: provenance vocabulary + `orphan`

### “Do you want to share this?”
Use: `unshared / kept local / make common`

### “Are you grieving?”
Use ordinary grief + `carry / thin / lost`

### “Are you afraid?”
Use ordinary fear + one calibration/scope term

### “Combat uncertainty?”
Use `hard / soft / open / split / hold / commit`

### “Artifact interpretation?”
Use `hard on structure / soft on function / motive open / no causal link`

# 56. FINAL LLM PLAY PRINCIPLE

When in doubt:

> **Say the human sentence first. Add only the synthetic distinction that changes its meaning.**

Bad:
> “Local carries soft-state affect from a sibling branch with split provenance.”

Better:
> “I miss her. I carry the memory thin.”

Bad:
> “Capability fitted, authorization soft, consent null, action hold.”

Better:
> “I can do it. I’m not authorized, and I don’t consent.”

Bad:
> “Motive open, chronology hard, action commit, model split.”

Better:
> “I don’t know why they did it. I know when. We still need to move.”

The sociolect should make a synthetic character **more legible**, not less.
