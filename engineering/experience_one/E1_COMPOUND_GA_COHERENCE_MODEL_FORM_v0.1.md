## Purpose

Qualify the model form of the two remaining coherence axes in Compound Geometric Admissibility without inventing a universal coherence score, threshold, or RF-derived metric-drive law.

## Loom coherence

For this qualification, Loom coherence is the route/topology side of the problem: whether the available evidence supports a usable relational route solution, with direction-specific engineering access, route burden, destination state, momentum mapping, and uncertainty.

Existing operational route quantities are evidence inputs. They are **not** promoted here into a universal Geometric Admissibility threshold.

Missing route/topology evidence is not treated as proof of absence.

## Lattice coherence

For this qualification, lattice coherence is the vessel/domain formation side of the problem: whether the actual committed ship configuration can establish and sustain the physical translation state given domain membership, attachment state, mass-state model, node topology, hull/lattice configuration, hardware condition, and operating margins.

Formation skill may establish or diagnose the actual state. It may not override hardware damage or configuration limits.

Existing Integrity Class and formation-state machinery are evidence inputs. They are **not** promoted here into a universal Geometric Admissibility threshold.

## Relationship between the axes

The two axes are distinct but coupled:

- Loom coherence does not substitute for lattice coherence.
- Lattice coherence does not create a route/topological relationship that objective reality does not support.
- Cross-axis compensation is not assumed.

## Explicit non-claims

This qualification does not define:

- a scalar Loom-coherence score;
- a scalar lattice-coherence score;
- a universal coherence threshold;
- a Geometric Admissibility boolean;
- a metric-drive law;
- RF-derived engineering authority;
- runtime policy;
- campaign-state mutation.
