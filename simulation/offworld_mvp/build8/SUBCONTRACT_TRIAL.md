# Build 8 one-level subcontract trial (research, opt-in)

`run_screened(subcontract=False)` preserves the existing path exactly. When enabled, the existing Northrop Grumman BUILD prime contracts a one-year landing-services package from the existing Firefly Aerospace LAND executor. Both prime work and subcontract work reserve slots in the **same** `occupied[(category, year)]` schedule used by direct jobs. No new executor identity or separate capacity pool is introduced. The subcontract consumes one LAND slot for one year **booked at BUILD start**. This is an intentionally coarse stand-in for some landing-related work, **not an actual landing or a realistic launch/landing schedule**; it is not a claim that a lunar landing has occurred. Only the original LAND stage can produce `ON_MOON`.

The subcontractor is fixed to Northrop Grumman → Firefly Aerospace and the package to 40% of BUILD base cost (0.20 model currency per project). It replaces that portion of the prime's expense: prime pays sub from received Sponsor revenue; remaining 60% goes to its ordinary expense account. Sponsor-to-prime payment and quoted price do not change. `BUILD8_SUB_PAYMENT` carries the real transfer ID, and all balances and boundary accounts remain under the existing kernel invariants. The subcontractor's receipt is revenue; no invented additional subcontractor cost is assumed. Accordingly, do not interpret the prime's old cost/profit presentation as a consolidated corporate profit measure when subcontracting is enabled.

A failed subcontract is paid 90% of its quote, withholding 10% under the same acceptance-holdback convention used for failed prime contracts; the withheld amount stays with the prime. The prime expense is the retained 60% of original BUILD cost, and the prime keeps the withheld 10% as cash. Subcontract `FAILED` blocks the prime's BUILD deliverable and hence LAND. With `resource_on_failure=True`, the prime instead self-performs the missing package, adding one year **after the reserved subcontract service slot has completed**; this is **not** another subcontract and does not bypass a supplier's capacity. The re-source is an authored option, not a search over multiple bidders. A sub whose slot is occupied waits for availability; if its completion is later than the prime's normal finish, prime acceptance waits. No deeper tiers are allowed: only the primary BUILD stage can create the one subcontract.

Knowledge receipts at subcontract start go only to prime and sub, both by `DIRECT_CONTRACT`. The Sponsor knows its direct BUILD contract and delivery result but does not automatically know about the subcontract. Identity guard rejects the prime, sub, or Sponsor being the same company for this trial. The existing knowledge list remains a trial-side audit, not part of the kernel fingerprint.

The optional scenario changes only the Build 8 screened delivery experiment. This is **not** a general-purpose contract graph, full construction supply chain, or canon runtime. It does not alter Build 7 or the shared kernel. An explicit opt-in would be needed to apply it to additional delivery chains.

## Decision register and future competition

The two review decisions, alternatives, rationale, and revisit triggers are recorded as `B8_SUB_SCHEDULE_001` and `B8_SUB_PAYMENT_002` in the **existing** `simulation/offworld_mvp/phase3b/MVP_ABSTRACTION_REGISTER_CANDIDATE_0_1.md`, rather than creating a new register.

Allowing any executor to subcontract to any other would require an authored list of eligible service packages, per-executor capabilities and shared capacity by service/year, plus explicit legal-entity identity checking. Several competing bidders would additionally require requests/quotes and a deterministic selection rule constrained by price, delivery time, and available slots. None is needed for the present two-company test, so none has been built.
